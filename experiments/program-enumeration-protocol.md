## Question and distinct outputs

Every earlier experiment measured one hand-written procedure. This experiment asks: among all programs of one small typed decision language up to a size bound, which decide satisfiability on a fixed corpus of n-variable formulas, how small is the smallest, and how fast is the fastest by declared events? It is a finite, exhaustive prefix of Levin-style enumeration by program length, run against a labelled corpus rather than against a verifier for one instance, so it is program synthesis from labelled examples at toy scale, in the spirit of Wolfram's enumeration of simple programs. If a program that decides the corpus quickly at small n exists in this grammar within the bound, enumeration finds it; the experiment says nothing about programs outside the grammar or above the bound.

Two curves are reported for each n:

1. **Minimum size:** the smallest node count of a program that decides every known-labelled formula of the corpus for n.
2. **Minimum worst-case cost:** over those programs, the smallest value of the maximum declared events on one known-labelled corpus formula, with the in-language search program R, its polarity twin, the exhaustive truth-table reference, and the existing DPLL solver as descriptive reference points.

The registered expectation is that R and its polarity twin are the only survivors. Because the bound admits R (15 nodes) but neither a program with unit propagation (19 nodes at least) nor any clause-learning procedure, the second curve can differ from R's cost only by the choice among 15-node programs; the protocol says so here, states R's cost analytically where a closed form exists, pre-registers the design-time model's values elsewhere, and treats the curve as a table to be checked against those predictions, not as evidence about growth. No required result is anything else; the absence of survivors is reported with the same completeness as their presence.

## The decision language

The calibration language of `src/lib.rs` returns a formula, never a decision, and cannot branch or recurse. Deciding SAT needs four additions: a Boolean result type, two formula tests, a literal-binding form for branching, and recursion. Each is a fixed, charged, decision-free primitive.

```text
Program ::= D                                   ; self names the whole program
D ::= true | false
    | (empty? F) | (conflict? F)
    | (if D D D)
    | (with-unit F D D)                         ; binds unit in the second argument
    | (with-literal F D D)                      ; binds lit in the second argument
    | (self F)
F ::= input | (with-unit F F) | (assign F L)    ; with-unit binds unit in its body
L ::= unit | lit | (neg L)
```

Types are Decision, Formula, and Literal; the parser rejects wrong types and arities, and `unit` and `lit` outside a binding form. `(assign F L)` is `(rewrite F L true true)`, the unique calibration survivor; its three sibling variants are excluded from the enumeration grammar: the identity variant never decreases the literal count, so the termination rule below forbids recursing on it; `(rewrite F L true false)` turns `(x) and (not x)` satisfiable; and `(rewrite F L false true)` with chosen literal `not x` turns `(not x or y) and (x)` unsatisfiable. Neither is a restriction of its source. A program that uses them in some other role is outside this grammar, which is a stated restriction of the search space. The parser accepts `rewrite` with explicit flags for the focused tests. The S-expression reader is the tokenizer of `lib::parse` and the reader `read` with `SExpr` of `src/lib.rs`, factored out and made `pub(crate)` by the implementation change; the decision language has its own typed AST.

**Scoping.** `input` always names the formula of the current `self` frame: the case formula in the first frame, the argument of the `self` that opened the frame afterwards. Decision-level `(with-unit F A B)` and `(with-literal F A B)` bind only `unit` or `lit`; they never rebind `input`, so A and B see the frame formula even when F is not `input`. Formula-level `(with-unit F G)` evaluates G with `input` rebound to the evaluated F and `unit` bound, exactly as the calibration evaluator does; G has type Formula and can contain no `self`, so no `self` ever sees a rebound `input`. The comparison in `self` is against the frame formula. A focused test pins this reading. An outer binding stays visible inside B; only A receives the new binding.

**`with-literal` and conflicts.** With 15 nodes and a `with-literal` that only looks for a nonempty clause, every survivor lacks conflict detection and R's frame count is `2^(n+1) - 1` on every corpus by construction, so the cost curve would be an artefact of the fallback semantics. Raising the bound to 19 nodes (the smallest program with early conflict detection under that semantics) is excluded by the replay bound; the pre-registered semantics below is the alternative. Decision-level `(with-literal F A B)` scans the clauses of the evaluated F in order for an empty clause; if it finds one, or if there are no clauses, it evaluates B with no new binding; otherwise it binds `lit` to the first literal of the first clause, which is nonempty because the scan found no empty clause, and evaluates A. B therefore handles both base cases and `(empty? input)` tells them apart. `conflict?` stays in the grammar so that the enumeration counts and order are unchanged from the recurrence below; it is redundant with the scan for programs that branch through `with-literal`.

**Event categories.** Every charge below is one of the checked categories of the `Work` vector of `src/transfer.rs`, named by its JSON field and listed here in the `Work::json` order, which is the order every `Work` vector in the artifact uses: `work_units` first, then formula check (`formula_checks`), clause read (`clause_reads`), literal read (`literal_reads`), clause write (`clause_writes`), literal write (`literal_writes`), `assignments`, `pair_checks`, rule attempt (`rule_attempts`), search node (`search_nodes`). As in the earlier protocols, a scalar read or write (a Boolean, a literal count, a bound literal) is charged to `literal_reads` or `literal_writes`. `assignments` and `pair_checks` are zero for every program evaluation and nonzero only in the truth-table reference and the DPLL reference point, which are outside every evaluation. `work_units` is the sum of the nine fields and is the number every "events", "cost", and "worst case" below refers to.

**Charges, in the order they are ticked.** Every evaluated node charges one rule attempt on entry, the analogue of the calibration language's AST visit; its children are charged when evaluated, each as its own node. A child written F evaluates to a formula value; every formula value carries its literal-occurrence count, maintained by the writes below, so that `self` reads it instead of recounting.

- Case validation, once per evaluation of one program on one formula and inside that evaluation's total, never repeated per frame: one clause read per clause, one literal read per literal, charged as `transfer::validate` charges, then one literal write recording the literal count. The evaluator re-implements the validation because `transfer::validate` is `pub(crate)` and rejects more than 12 variables; the charges are identical.
- `true`, `false`: the rule attempt only.
- `input`: one formula check; the value is the frame formula, not copied.
- `(empty? F)`: F; one formula check; true when F has no clauses.
- `(conflict? F)`: F; one formula check; one clause read per clause in order up to and including the first empty clause, every clause when there is none; true when one exists.
- `(if C A B)`: C; then one literal read of the Boolean, charged after C returns; then exactly one of A and B.
- Decision-level `(with-unit F A B)`: F; one formula check; one clause read per clause in order up to and including the first singleton; if found, one literal read of its literal and one literal write for the binding, then A with `unit` bound; otherwise B with no new binding.
- Decision-level `(with-literal F A B)`: F; one formula check; one clause read per clause in order up to and including the first empty clause, every clause when there is none; if an empty clause was found or there are no clauses, B with no new binding; otherwise one literal read of the first literal of the first clause and one literal write for the binding, then A with `lit` bound.
- Formula-level `(with-unit F G)`: F; one clause read per clause in order up to and including the first singleton; if found, one literal read of its literal and one literal write for the binding, then G with `input` rebound to the evaluated F and `unit` bound; otherwise one formula check and the value is the evaluated F. The differences from `src/lib.rs` are three: the rule attempt in place of the AST visit, the literal write for the binding, and the formula check when no singleton is found; the absence of a copy is a difference of the `input` node, not of this form. The focused test states its exact deltas against `lib.rs` in the test section below.
- `unit`, `lit`: one literal read.
- `(neg L)`: L; one literal write.
- `(assign F L)`: F; L; one literal write for the opposite literal; then for each clause of the evaluated F in order: one clause read, one literal read per literal scanned until the chosen literal is found (every literal when it is absent); a clause containing the chosen literal is dropped; otherwise one clause write, then per literal one literal read and, when the literal is not the opposite literal, one literal write keeping it; finally one literal write recording the result's literal count. These are the `rewrite` charges of `src/lib.rs` with both flags true, minus the copy that `lib.rs` charges for `input`, plus the two literal writes (the opposite literal and the result count), and plus the one literal read that `unit` or `lit` charges here where `lib.rs` charges only an AST visit; the focused test states its exact deltas against `lib.rs` in the test section below and does not compare rule attempts or formula checks.
- `(self F)`: F, evaluated to G; one search node; two literal reads of the literal counts of G and of the frame formula; if G's count is strictly smaller, the whole program is evaluated in a new frame with `input` bound to G and `unit` and `lit` unbound, and the frame's decision is the value; otherwise evaluation stops with the outcome `invalid`.

**Termination, proved on this class.** Premises: every program is a finite tree of the grammar above, and `self` opens a frame only when the argument's literal count is strictly smaller than the frame formula's. Every frame's formula is derived from the frame formula by `assign` and rebinding, and an `assign` whose literal's variable occurs in its source removes every occurrence of that variable, while an `assign` whose variable is absent changes nothing; so a strict decrease of the literal count implies that the new frame's formula lacks at least one variable of the old one, and the depth of frames is at most n. Within one frame the evaluation is a single walk that visits each node at most once. Hence every program terminates on every input. `invalid` is a defined outcome of the semantics, not a resource limit: the program fails to decide the formula, and it refutes the program in the staging below. R and DPLL are expressible because `assign` on the bound literal, which occurs in the frame formula, removes at least one occurrence. The event budget below is a measurement bound, not the termination argument.

**Why this does not smuggle in a solver.** `empty?`, `conflict?`, and the scan inside `with-literal` are the base cases any branching decision must be able to state and read only the clause structure; `with-literal` chooses the first literal of the first clause, a fixed syntactic choice with no lookahead; `self` is plain recursion with a well-foundedness check; `assign` is the calibration rule. None inspects labels, models, or counts, and none returns a decision on its own: `(with-literal input true false)` is wrong on `(x) and (not x)`, and `(with-literal input true (empty? input))` answers `true` on every conflict-free nonempty formula and is refuted by the full cube.

**Size and canonical text.** The size of a program is its node count: every atom and every operator application counts one. The canonical text of a program is its one-line S-expression in the form `fmt::Display` of `src/lib.rs` prints for `FormulaExpr` and `LiteralExpr` (`input`, `unit`, `(neg {x})`, `(with-unit {source} {body})`), extended to the decision constructs the same way: an atom is its name; an application is an opening parenthesis, the operator name, then each child in order, every two adjacent tokens separated by exactly one space, and a closing parenthesis; no other whitespace and no newline; `assign` is printed as `assign`, never as its `rewrite` expansion. Every program text in this document is canonical, except that the two displays of R below are wrapped for reading and are canonical once each line break with its indentation is replaced by one space. The canonical text is what the per-size digests hash, what every survivor `text` field carries, and what the round-trip test re-parses. R is

```lisp
(with-literal input
  (if (self (assign input lit)) true (self (assign input (neg lit))))
  (empty? input))
```

with 15 nodes. It branches on both polarities of the first literal of the first clause, returns `false` when a conflict has appeared, and `true` when no clause remains. Its polarity twin tries `(neg lit)` first:

```lisp
(with-literal input
  (if (self (assign input (neg lit))) true (self (assign input lit)))
  (empty? input))
```

DPLL with unit propagation, `(with-unit input (self (assign input unit)) R)`, has 21 nodes; the formula-level substitution `(with-unit input (assign input unit))` for one `input` of R gives a 19-node variant. Both lie outside the bound.

## Enumeration order and count

Let σ record which of `unit`, `lit` are bound. Gen(τ, s, σ) is the sequence obtained by iterating the productions of type τ in the order displayed in the grammar; a production with one child takes that child from Gen at size s−1; a production with several children iterates over the compositions of s−1 into positive parts in ascending lexicographic order with the first child's size outermost, and for each composition over the Cartesian product of the children's sequences with the first child outermost. Atoms come before applications; `unit` precedes `lit`; `true` precedes `false`. Programs are enumerated by size ascending and, within a size, in the order Gen(D, s, ∅). The rank of a program is its zero-based position within its size, and `s:rank` identifies it in the artifact. Ties between programs anywhere in this protocol are broken by size ascending, then rank ascending. The order defines the sequence; the enumerator produces programs lazily in that order (by direct recursion or by unranking from the count table), evaluates and hashes each program as it is produced, and materializes no size as a list.

The count per size follows the recurrence, with [·] the indicator and sums over positive parts:

```text
L(1) = [unit bound] + [lit bound];              L(s) = L(s-1)
F(1) = 1;   F(s) = Σ_{a+b=s-1} F(a)·F(b; unit bound) + Σ_{a+b=s-1} F(a)·L(b)
D(1) = 2;   D(s) = 3·F(s-1) + Σ_{a+b+c=s-1} [ D(a)D(b)D(c) + F(a)·D(b; unit bound)·D(c) + F(a)·D(b; lit bound)·D(c) ]
```

evaluated with the scope flags of the enclosing form, in checked `u64` (every summand is at most the total, which fits). Programs per size 1 to 15 are 2, 3, 0, 19, 60, 89, 446, 2,220, 5,545, 19,427, 94,681, 323,947, 1,084,410, 4,750,985, 18,672,125; cumulatively 24,953,959, of which 446,439 have size at most 12. The enumerator must produce exactly these counts, without duplicates. Uniqueness is checked by set membership of canonical texts through size 12 in `cargo test`; above size 12 the texts are not held in memory, and uniqueness follows from the count match together with the unambiguity of the grammar: every canonical text has exactly one derivation, the generator emits one program per derivation, and the recurrence counts derivations, so with the counts matching a duplicate would force a missing program; the generator is one function at every size, verified by set membership through size 12, and the count match is the evidence above 12 that it still emits one program per derivation there. The artifact records, per size, the SHA-256 of the concatenation of the canonical texts of that size's programs in rank order, each text followed by one newline; the digest of the empty size 3 is the SHA-256 of the empty string, `e3b0c44298fc1c149afbf4c8996fb92427ae41e4649b934ca495991b7852b855`. SHA-256 is the std-only FIPS 180-4 implementation of `src/sha256.rs`, tested on the NIST vectors, used in streaming form so no size is held in memory; no crate.

The bound is S_max = 15, the size of R, and every size from 1 to 15 is mandatory: a run that does not complete size 15 produces no artifact, and no optional size exists, so every claim below ranges over sizes 1 to 15 exactly. The bound is set by the replay-time rule below (size 16 would add 66,564,638 programs, about 2.7 times the whole enumeration through size 15 and about 3.6 times size 15 alone; the smallest program with unit propagation needs size 19); raising it, with or without pruning, requires a new protocol identity. Sizes are completed in ascending order; within a size, programs are evaluated in rank order, single-threaded, so the artifact is deterministic without a reduction step.

## Fixed corpus per n

For n from 3 to 14 the gate corpus T_n is enumerated in the order below. Random clauses use the fragment-interface generator unchanged (`effective_seed`, `draw`, `random` of `src/fragment.rs`): xorshift64 `x ^= x << 13; x ^= x >> 7; x ^= x << 17` on a 64-bit unsigned state with truncating shifts, the transition applied before each value is read, initial state `s XOR (n << 32) XOR (t << 40) XOR (b << 48)`, clauses of width w drawn by repeated variable draws `next() % n + 1` with rejection of a variable already in the clause and, once accepted, a sign from a fresh draw's low bit with zero meaning positive, `t*n` ternary clauses then `b` binary clauses from one generator, repeats allowed.

- **`cube-exhaustive` (n = 3 only):** the eight width-3 clauses over variables 1, 2, 3, indexed 0 to 7 where bit i of the index set negates variable i+1, literals in variable order; every subset by mask, clauses in ascending index, in the order mask 0 (the empty formula, satisfiable), mask 255 (the full cube, the only unsatisfiable subset, since every assignment falsifies exactly one of the eight clauses), then masks 1 to 254 ascending. 256 formulas. The order puts the two cheapest refuters of constant and near-constant programs first; it changes where a program is refuted, never whether.
- **`random`:** base seeds `5003, 10007, 20011` outermost, then ternary densities `3, 4, 5`, then binary counts `0, n`. 18 cases, labelled by binary count. At n = 3 these are multisets of the eight cube clauses, plus three binary clauses when b = 3, so they add repetition and clause order to `cube-exhaustive`, not new structure.
- **`parity-cycle`:** for i from 1 to n with `next(i) = i mod n + 1`, the pair `(i or next(i))`, `(not i or not next(i))` in that order; unsatisfiable exactly when n is odd.
- **`cube-embedded` (n ≥ 4):** the eight width-3 clauses over variables n−2, n−1, n in ascending index with the same sign convention, declared over n variables; unsatisfiable. At n = 3 it is mask 255 and is omitted.
- **`chain-unsat` and `chain-sat`:** `(1)`, then `(not i or i+1)` for i from 1 to n−1, then `(not n)`; and the same without the final unit. Both are 2-CNF with units, decided by unit propagation alone and in P; together with the parity cycles they measure what a program's fallback semantics costs on easy structure, not SAT hardness. Under the semantics above R follows the chain on `chain-sat` in n+1 frames because first-literal branching coincides with propagation on this clause order, and refutes `chain-unsat` in 2n+1 frames because each backtrack meets a conflict at once.
- **`pigeonhole`:** at n = 6 and n = 12 only, the existing encoding of `src/transfer.rs` with variable `p*holes + h` for pigeon p from 0 and hole h from 1: the pigeon clauses first in pigeon order, then for each hole in order and each pair `a < b` of pigeons the clause `(not (a*holes+h) or not (b*holes+h))`: PHP(3,2) and PHP(4,3), unsatisfiable. Pigeonhole formulas are never sent to the oracle: they occur only at n ≤ 12, where the truth table labels them.

T_n therefore has 22 formulas, 23 at n = 6 and n = 12, and 277 at n = 3. The held-out corpus H_n has the **`held-random`** family, base seeds `40009, 80021` with the same density and binary-count grid in the same nesting, 12 cases, followed for n ≥ 4 by **`cube-low`**, the eight width-3 clauses over variables 1, 2, 3 in ascending index with the same sign convention, declared over n variables, unsatisfiable; 13 formulas, 12 at n = 3. `cube-low` repeats the construction of `cube-embedded` at the other end of the variable range and tests only variable-position invariance; H_3 has no structured control. Every corpus clause has width at most three, which the truth-table reference below requires.

**Case ids.** Every case is keyed by an id following the `family-n{n}` precedent of `src/transfer.rs` and `src/implication.rs`, with decimal numbers and no padding: `cube-exhaustive-m{mask}` for mask 0 to 255 (n = 3 only); `random-n{n}-t{t}-b{b}-s{seed}` with the ternary density t, the binary count b, and the base seed; `parity-cycle-n{n}`; `cube-embedded-n{n}`; `chain-unsat-n{n}` and `chain-sat-n{n}`; `pigeonhole-3-2` and `pigeonhole-4-3`; `held-random-n{n}-t{t}-b{b}-s{seed}`; `cube-low-n{n}`. The family is the id's prefix before its first parameter. Ids are unique within T_n and H_n together, since the held-out seeds differ from the gate seeds; `worst.formula`, `unknown` rows, `held_out.first_disagreement`, `held_out.first_unknown`, and the corpus rows use these ids and nothing else.

**Expected labels (pre-freeze).** The design-time model gives satisfiable counts in T_n of 269 of 277 at n = 3 and 12, 14, 10, 11, 8, 13, 11, 8, 12, 11, 8 of 22 (23 at n = 6 and 12) for n = 4 to 14, and in H_n of 8 of 12 at n = 3 and 10, 6, 7, 7, 6, 5, 4, 5, 7, 6, 3 of 13 for n = 4 to 14. The artifact records the counts per n; the label-count check under "Implementation checks" below compares them, since the corpus is deterministic, with an oracle unknown at n = 13 or 14 reducing the known count and never counting as a disagreement. Constant programs are refuted at the first or second formula of T_3 (mask 0 is satisfiable, mask 255 is not), so no constant reaches stage 4.

**Freshness.** Reject exact `(declared_variables, ordered_CNF)` overlap within and between T_n and H_n, and between every case of T_n and H_n and every case of every earlier corpus (implication-calibration, clue-transfer, indexed-transfer, fragment-interface-v1, extraction-cost-v1, and any corpus frozen before this protocol's freeze commit), structured and random families alike; a hit outside the listed shared controls invalidates the corpus. The design-time model found the 676 formulas of all T_n and H_n pairwise distinct (pre-freeze); the check against the earlier corpora runs in the freeze commit and its result is recorded there. The shared controls are exempt from earlier-corpus rejection, listed explicitly, and reported as shared, each with the corpus it comes from: `parity-cycle` at n = 3 to 12 from implication-calibration (`PARITY_SIZES` of `src/implication.rs`) and at n = 5 to 12 from clue-transfer (`src/transfer.rs`); `pigeonhole-3-2` and `pigeonhole-4-3` from clue-transfer; `cube-embedded` at n = 6, 8, 10, 12 from clue-transfer's `ternary-cube` family. `cube-exhaustive`, `cube-embedded` at the other n, `cube-low`, `parity-cycle` at n = 13 and 14, the chains, and every random case are new. All sizes are calibration sizes; they are not scaling evidence. The order of T_n affects only where a wrong program is refuted, never which programs survive or their worst-case cost.

## Reference model

The artifact's `reference_model` string is `truth-table<=12;definition;construction;oracle:cadical-3.0.1+model-check+lrat-check`, defined as `enumeration::REFERENCE_MODEL` in `src/enumeration.rs`. It is not `oracle::REFERENCE_MODEL`, whose value `truth-table<=12;definition;oracle:cadical-3.0.1+model-check+lrat-check` and its JavaScript twin in `scripts/oh-report-checks.mjs` stay unchanged; this experiment's ledger validator compares `reference_model` against the protocol string. The added `construction` segment is listed under the amendments below.

**Truth table, n ≤ 12.** The reference is `oracle::label`, which at or below 12 variables calls `implication::reference_wide`, the truth-table evaluator admitting clauses of width at most three: it enumerates all 2^n assignments and records the model count, the backbone, and the label with its declared enumeration work, which is outside every evaluation. Every structured family with a theorem label (`parity-cycle`, `cube-exhaustive` mask 255, `cube-embedded`, `cube-low`, the chains, `pigeonhole`) is also checked against its construction label below; the case row carries the result beside its reference object as `construction_check: {family, valid, work}` (`null` for a family without a theorem label), with the structural check's work on the checker meter; a disagreement stops the run.

**Definition cases, n = 13 and 14.** A formula with an empty clause or with no clauses is labelled in Rust as `oracle::label` does. No corpus formula at n = 13 or 14 has either shape, so the expected count of definition cases is zero; the reporting path exists and is tested.

**Construction labels, n = 13 and 14.** Structured families whose label is a theorem carry their label by construction, with a cited proof and a structural check in Rust, and are never sent to the oracle. The structural check regenerates the family's clause list for n and requires equality with the case formula, charging one clause read per clause and one literal read per literal on the checker meter. `parity-cycle` at odd n is unsatisfiable: each pair `(i or j)`, `(not i or not j)` is the equation `x_i + x_j = 1` modulo 2, and summing the n equations around the cycle, where every variable occurs twice, gives `0 = n mod 2`, a contradiction for odd n (Tseitin's formulas on a cycle with odd charge; Urquhart 1987 for the general construction). `parity-cycle` at even n is satisfiable with the alternating model `x_i = [i odd]`, checked by `check_model`. `cube-embedded` and `cube-low` are unsatisfiable because every assignment to the three variables falsifies exactly the clause whose sign pattern it complements. `chain-unsat` is unsatisfiable by unit propagation from `(1)` to `(n)` against `(not n)`; `chain-sat` is satisfiable with the all-true model, checked by `check_model`. A satisfiable construction label therefore always carries a checked model, an ordinary certificate; an unsatisfiable one carries the family name, the citation, and the structural check. The reference kind is `construction`; the row records `kind`, `label`, `family`, `proof` (the citation string), `structural_check: {valid, work}`, and, for SAT, `model` and `model_check` in the oracle design's shapes. In Rust the experiment's `enumeration::label` wraps `oracle::label`: a construction family above 12 variables takes the construction path, every other case calls `oracle::label` unchanged, and the experiment's own label enum holds either an `oracle::Label` or a construction record; `oracle::Label` is not modified.

**Oracle, n = 13 and 14.** The random cases of T_13, T_14, H_13, and H_14, 30 per n and 60 in all, are labelled by the declared oracle: CaDiCaL 3.0.1 at `/opt/homebrew/bin/cadical`, pinned binary SHA-256 `601c9fa8ba5d09fd81bb00c89b3e54832f138bccc3422bd8652e8cda4d74d1fa`, invocation `cadical -q --lrat --binary=false -c 100000 <input.cnf> <proof.lrat>`, conflict limit N = 100,000, the only oracle limit, never a time limit. A SAT answer is accepted only after the model is checked against the raw clauses by `check_model`; an UNSAT answer only after the std-only LRAT checker of `src/lrat.rs` accepts the proof (RUP with hints, deletions honoured, strictly increasing lemma ids from m+1, final empty clause required); both checks are charged on a checker meter outside every evaluation and reported separately. The per-entry proof cap is 2,097,152 bytes and the answer-file cap is 16,777,216 bytes; both are sizing constraints. The file cap binds first (60 × 2,097,152 = 125,829,120 bytes exceeds it), and neither is expected to bind: the merged reference oracle's only measured proof on random 3-SAT, `tests/oracle/p60.lrat` at n = 60, is 5,604 bytes with 89 lemmas, larger than any formula here, and even if all 60 entries carried a proof of that size the file would hold 336,240 bytes of proof text and be under 400 KB in all; the expectation is 60 entries, no definition entries, no composed calls, and a file well under 1 MB. The committed answer file is also recorded in the ledger as the edition `edition:program-enumeration-oracle-<sha256 of file bytes>` that the evidence record depends on, as `docs/oh.md` states, with the evidence fields `oracleModel`, `oracleCalls`, `oracleUnknown`, `proofLemmasTotal`, and `answersSha256`; that edition is one record under the pinned 1 MiB record cap, a tighter bound than the 16,777,216-byte file cap, which this protocol adopts as its own answer-file bound for ledger admission; an answer file above it is recorded as an ordered split of its `entries` array in the form of `commitInParts`, an adapter change that alters no artifact value. There are no composed calls: no backbone, no sampling, no query beyond the one label per formula. A conflict-limit unknown, or a proof over the cap, labels the case `unknown`: the case stays in the artifact with the survivors' outcomes on it, is listed in every per-family table, is excluded from every comparison, curve, and stage decision, and is never re-drawn. An invalid model, a rejected proof, a parse failure, a missing, duplicate, or unused answer, a digest or version mismatch of the binary during regeneration, or a limit or identity mismatch in the answer file is an error that stops the run and names the case digest, exactly as `oracle::label` and the answer-file checks of `src/oracle.rs` do; none of these is ever a label.

**Oracle-answer file and replay.** `artifacts/program-enumeration-oracle.json` is written only by `write_answers` in the oracle design's fixed serialization: entries keyed and sorted by `dimacs_sha256`, no case ids, models for SAT, full LRAT text for UNSAT, the conflict limit, and the pinned identity; the artifact links each case to its entry by digest. Replay regenerates the corpus, recomputes each DIMACS digest with `write_dimacs`, looks up the entry, and labels the case from the re-verified answer; `bun run check` runs replay on every machine and must reproduce `artifacts/program-enumeration.json` byte for byte. Where the pinned binary is present with a matching digest, the gate also regenerates the answer file and requires byte identity, naming the first differing entry; `PEQNP_REQUIRE_ORACLE=1` makes a skipped regeneration a failure and is the final gate for any change to the answer file. Timing goes to the uncommitted sidecar `artifacts/program-enumeration-timing.json`, never compared and never cited. The artifact carries no wall-clock seconds and no solver-reported conflict counts.

**Driver order.** The oracle design's driver looks up a case's answer only after every arm has returned for that case. This experiment's arms are 24,953,959 programs evaluated fail-fast, which needs the label of each formula while the programs are still being evaluated on it. Labels for T_n and H_n are therefore looked up and verified once, after the corpus is fixed and before stage n begins, by the driver alone; the program evaluator has the signature `(program, input, n, budget)`, imports neither `oracle` nor `lrat`, and receives no label, model, count, or proof; a focused test with a recording fake proves that no evaluation reads a label. The guarantee the design's ordering exists for, that no oracle value reaches an arm, is kept by construction. This departure is listed under the amendments below.

**Artifact contents per case.** For every corpus formula: `id`, `family`, `variables` (the declared n), `input` (the clause list), `dimacs_sha256`, `label` (`sat`, `unsat`, or `unknown`), `shared` (`null` or the earlier corpus name), `reference` (the tagged reference object in the oracle design's shapes: `truth-table` with model count, backbone, and work; `definition`; `construction` as above; `oracle` with `conflict_limit`, the verified model and `model_check` for SAT, `proof_check` with `lrat_sha256`, `lrat_bytes`, `lemmas`, `deletions`, and work for UNSAT, `reason` for unknown, and the identity), and `construction_check` as above; `input` and `variables` are the names `oracleReference` reads. `construction_check` is `null` on every row whose reference kind is `construction`, whose structural check lives inside the reference object, and on every row of a family without a theorem label. The report declares `reference_model`, `oracle_limits: {conflicts: 100000}`, `proof_text_cap_bytes`, and `oracle_answers: {path, sha256}`, where `path` is always the registered relative path `artifacts/program-enumeration-oracle.json` (`oracle::answers_path("program-enumeration")`) regardless of the `--oracle-answers` argument, as `demo_replay` writes it, and `sha256` is the digest of the file's bytes. The reference summary is an experiment-local object of `src/enumeration.rs` whose fields are those of `oracle::Summary::json` (`cases`, `truth_table_cases`, `definition_cases`, `oracle_cases`, `oracle_calls`, `oracle_sat`, `oracle_unsat`, `oracle_unknown`, `model_check_work_units`, `proof_check_work_units`, `proof_lemmas_total`, `reference_work_units`, `enumeration_ratio`) plus `construction_cases`, with `cases` equal to the corpus size 676, `reference_work_units` summed over truth-table rows only, and `enumeration_ratio` always `null`, because the oracle design defines it as reference work over an arm's total and this experiment has no arm total; `oracle::Summary` itself is unchanged.

## Amendments to the reference-oracle design

Each item below departs from the merged reference-oracle design and needs reference-oracle review per `AGENTS.md`, independently of this protocol's review, before the freeze commit.

1. **A `construction` label source and reference-model segment.** The design labels every case above 12 variables by definition or by the oracle; this protocol labels the structured families at n = 13 and 14 by construction with a cited proof, a structural check, and a checked model for SAT, and declares the segment `construction` in its `reference_model` string. `oracle::REFERENCE_MODEL`, `oracle::Label`, and `oracle::label` are unchanged; the experiment wraps them as stated above. The experiment's ledger validator, `scripts/oh-enumeration-report.mjs`, accepts a `construction` row (family in the listed set, `variables > 12`, the structural check re-run in JavaScript from the raw clauses, a SAT row's model satisfying every clause) and delegates every `truth-table`, `definition`, and `oracle` row to the shared `oracleReference` unchanged.
2. **Driver order.** The design's invariant "oracle values are consumed by replay after arms return" and its driver-order test are replaced, for this experiment only, by the rule that labels may be looked up before evaluation when the evaluator's signature provably receives no label and a recording fake proves that no lookup happens during evaluation; the recording-fake test in the tests section is the substitute for the design's driver-order test here.

## Budgets and accounting

Every evaluation of one program on one formula has the budget B(n) = 2^(n+15) events: 262,144 at n = 3, 134,217,728 at n = 12, 536,870,912 at n = 14. Exhaustion is `unknown`, never a decision and never a refutation. Events use the checked `Work` vector of the earlier experiments with unit increments and checked arithmetic; the per-formula total includes the case validation charges. Reference, oracle, checkers, enumeration, parsing, and reporting are outside every evaluation.

**The budget never binds, proved on this class.** A frame evaluates at most one path through the program, so at most 15 nodes; a third `self` on one path needs an `if` above the second `self`, a literal binder, and a third `(assign input lit)` or `(with-unit input (assign input unit))`, at least 19 nodes in total, so a frame of a program of at most 15 nodes opens at most two child frames; the depth is at most n by the termination argument, so a program visits at most `2^(n+1) - 1` frames. Every node charges at most `3L + 2C + 3` events on a frame formula with C clauses and L literal occurrences, the maximum being `assign`, and no derived formula is larger than the case formula. Hence one evaluation charges at most `(2^(n+1) - 1) · 15 · (3L + 2C + 3) + C + L + 1` events. The largest corpus formulas have `C = 6n` and `L = 17n` (density 5 with n binary clauses), giving at n = 14 a bound of 434,982,248 against B(14) = 536,870,912, and the inequality `15 · (63n + 3) < 2^14` holds for every n ≤ 17. Therefore no evaluation on any corpus formula can exhaust B(n), U_n below is empty by proof, and no program is dismissed by budget; the budget is kept as a safety bound and the `unknown` path is exercised in the focused tests with an artificially small budget. An `unknown` in the run contradicts this argument or the charge table and is handled as the "No unknowns" implementation check states.

**Precondition.** Before enumeration, R and its polarity twin must each complete every known-labelled formula of T_n and H_n within B(n)/4 and agree with every known label there; if either does not, the budget is too small for the question or the interpreter or a label is defective, and the run stops without a result. The design-time model (pre-freeze) puts R's and the twin's worst case at n = 14 at 98,967 events, about 1/1,356 of B(14)/4; the run re-establishes it.

The rule attempt per node is interpretive overhead that neither the truth-table reference nor the DPLL reference point pays; every R and survivor cost is therefore reported both as `work_units` and as `work_units - rule_attempts`. The second figure is the maximum over formulas of the difference, with its own formula id, not the difference on the formula that maximizes `work_units`; the two may name different formulas. Wherever a "worst formula" is named, it is the first in corpus order among the formulas attaining the maximum. The DPLL and truth-table columns are descriptive only. The DPLL reference point is `solve(input, n, None, B(n))` of `src/transfer.rs`; it and the truth table are reported for n ≤ 12 only, because `transfer::validate` rejects more than 12 variables for both and lifting that cap is a reviewed evaluator change outside this protocol. The DPLL solver's worst case is the maximum of the arm's `total_work_units` (preprocessing plus residual) over the formulas of T_n it completed, with `unknown` the count it did not; the truth-table worst case is the maximum `work_units` of `implication::reference_wide` over T_n.

## Survivors, unknowns, held-out check, and the two curves

Evaluation is staged. C_2 is the set of all enumerated programs. Stage n runs every program of C_{n−1} on the known-labelled formulas of T_n in corpus order and stops at the first formula whose outcome is not the label: an outcome that is the wrong decision or `invalid` **refutes** the program at n; an outcome `unknown` places it in U_n, the **unknown at n** set, with the formula recorded. C_n is the set of programs correct on every known-labelled formula of T_n. A program in U_n is neither a survivor nor refuted; it is not evaluated at later stages and is reported at n with its size, rank, and the formula on which the budget ran out. Every program of C_n is additionally evaluated on the unknown-labelled formulas of T_n, if any, and its outcomes there are recorded without comparison. A program that survives stage n has survived every smaller stage; this is the uniform reading of the question. The set C_3 is also the set of programs correct on T_3 chosen independently of every other n, because every program is evaluated on T_3 first; for n ≥ 4 the non-uniform minimum, the smallest program correct on T_n alone, is not measured by this protocol and cannot be inferred from C_n, and no P/poly reading is claimed.

For each n the artifact reports |C_n|, |U_n| by size, and the number refuted by size and by outcome kind; the minimum size over C_n with every survivor of that size listed by `s:rank` and text in rank order; the minimum over C_n of the worst-case `work_units` on the known-labelled formulas of T_n with every program attaining it in size-then-rank order, its full `Work` vector on its worst formula, and that formula's id (first in corpus order among ties); the same two numbers for R and for the twin; the worst-case events of the truth-table reference and of the DPLL solver on T_n under B(n) for n ≤ 12, with `unknown` reported if the DPLL budget runs out; and every survivor's worst-case events with the id of its worst formula and its `total_work_units`, the sum over the known-labelled formulas of T_n. Survivors are listed raw; no normalization up to trivial rewrites is applied, because no such rewrite of R (for instance `(if X true false)` for X) fits within 15 nodes.

A program correct on T_n is not a SAT algorithm; it is correct on about two dozen formulas. Every program of C_n is then run on every formula of H_n with the same budget and no fail-fast: `held_out.work_units` has one entry per formula of H_n in corpus order, holding the events charged up to the stop when the outcome is `invalid` or `unknown`, and the outcomes on unknown-labelled formulas of H_n are recorded under `held_out.unknown_outcomes` without comparison. The artifact records agreement on the known-labelled formulas, the first formula whose outcome is not the label (a wrong decision or `invalid`), or the first `unknown` formula. Survivors that agree everywhere have `held_out.status` `bounded-tested`; survivors with a wrong decision or `invalid` on a known-labelled formula of H_n are `corpus-fitted`, remain in C_n for the curves, and are listed separately; survivors with an `unknown` on H_n and no disagreement are `held-out-unknown`. These three strings are the only values of `held_out.status`. For every program attaining the minimum worst case on T_n, and for R and the twin, the artifact also reports the worst-case `work_units` on the known-labelled formulas of H_n, the post-selection cost, next to the T_n value that selected it. Both curves are also reported restricted to the bounded-tested subset. Nothing about H_n reaches the enumeration or the stage decisions.

## Claims and falsification criteria

Every claim is a conjecture until the run completes; a claim that survives its criterion is bounded-tested on this range, never proved. Every statistic in this protocol is descriptive: there is no sampling model and no significance test, and no null hypothesis is stated because none is tested. None of the falsifiers below invalidates a run: a falsified claim is a reported result. Theorems, accounting identities, and consequences of the fixed corpus and charge table are not claims; they are the implementation checks in the acceptance section.

1. **Minimum size.** Conjecture: for every n from 3 to 14, C_n contains no program other than R and its polarity twin; in particular no program of size at most 14 is in C_n. Falsified by one survivor other than those two programs at any stage. Not established, and reported as such, if any program is in U_n for some n, since an unknown is not a refutation. The part of this claim at n = 3 for sizes 1 to 12 replicates pre-freeze observation (1); sizes 13 to 15 at n = 3 and every size at n from 4 to 14 are predictions. That R and the twin are in every C_n is not part of the claim: they are correct DPLL on every formula, their presence is the validity precondition, and their absence could only be an interpreter or label defect.
2. **Cost against R.** Conjecture: if some program other than R and the twin is in C_n, then at that n the minimum worst-case `work_units` over C_n is at least one half of R's worst-case `work_units` on the known-labelled formulas of T_n. Falsified at n by one survivor whose worst case is below half of R's. Vacuous, and reported as vacuous, at every n where C_n is exactly R and the twin; there the ratio column is the twin's, pre-registered above as 887/864 at n = 3 and 1 at n = 4 to 14. Comparisons count only formulas with known labels that every compared program completed.
3. **Held-out agreement.** Conjecture: if some program other than R and the twin is in C_n, the corpus-fitted count at n is zero. Falsified by one corpus-fitted survivor; such a survivor is a concrete demonstration of the overfitting risk and is reported as such. Vacuous, and reported as vacuous, where C_n is exactly R and the twin, which are correct on every formula.

## Reported quantities and rounding

Every reported count is an integer. Every non-integer quantity is a `RatioPair`: an exact rational pair of integers plus a thousandths string computed by integer arithmetic as `ratio` in `src/fragment.rs` and `ratio_pair` of the oracle module do: with numerator p and denominator q > 0, the string is `floor((1000·p + floor(q/2)) / q)` written as integer part, a point, and three digits; a zero denominator or a checked-arithmetic overflow prints `null`. No floating point appears in the artifact. The quantities so reported are: each survivor's worst case divided by R's worst case at the same n, once as `work_units` over `work_units` and once as `work_units - rule_attempts` over the same for R; the twin's worst case divided by R's, both ways; the truth-table reference's and the DPLL solver's worst case at n ≤ 12 divided by R's, once with R's `work_units` and once with R's `work_units - rule_attempts` as the denominator; and successive ratios r_n = c_{n+1}/c_n, computed only within families of fixed composition, never on the maximum over all of T_n, whose composition changes with n: the random family of T_n (`random`), `chain-sat`, `chain-unsat`, `cube-embedded`, `parity-cycle` on odd n (`parity-cycle-odd`) and on even n (`parity-cycle-even`) separately, each for R, for the twin, and for the minimum worst-case curve, for consecutive pairs whose two values are known. For R and the twin, c_n of a family is that program's maximum `work_units` over the family's known-labelled formulas of T_n. For the minimum worst-case curve, c_n of a family is the minimum over C_n of that per-program family maximum, a function of C_n alone; the program attaining it is listed by `s:rank`, ties by size then rank. A family value at n is unknown when the family has no known-labelled formula at n (an odd-n or even-n family at the other parity, or an oracle unknown), and pairs with an unknown value are omitted.

Each successive ratio is accompanied by two signed integer descriptors, computed in checked `u128`, printing `null` on overflow; both use one direct definition whether the pair increases or decreases, and no signed `RatioPair` is used anywhere in the artifact. The binary exponent e_n is the unique integer e, positive, zero, or negative, with c_n · 2^e ≤ c_{n+1} < c_n · 2^(e+1); for e ≥ 0 the test is c_n · 2^e ≤ c_{n+1} and for e < 0 it is c_n ≤ c_{n+1} · 2^(−e); the search starts at e = 0 and steps up while the test holds or down until it holds. The mantissa is c_{n+1} / (c_n · 2^e) for e ≥ 0 and (c_{n+1} · 2^(−e)) / c_n for e < 0, a `RatioPair` in [1, 2). The log-log slope bracket k_n is the largest integer k, positive, zero, or negative, with ((n+1)/n)^k ≤ c_{n+1}/c_n; for k ≥ 0 the test is (n+1)^k · c_n ≤ n^k · c_{n+1} and for k < 0 it is n^(−k) · c_n ≤ (n+1)^(−k) · c_{n+1}, with the same search from k = 0. Because ((n+1)/n)^k increases with k, the set of k passing the test is downward closed and the bracket is well defined, and the log-log slope log(c_{n+1}/c_n) / log((n+1)/n) lies in [k_n, k_n + 1) in every case. For example a ratio of 6/10 has e = −1 with mantissa 12/10, and a ratio of 9/10 at n = 10 has k = −2, since (10/11)^2 ≤ 9/10 < 10/11. e_n and k_n are the only signed numbers in the artifact; the experiment's validator checks them as signed safe integers, since the shared `count` and `integersOnly` checks of `scripts/oh-report-checks.mjs` accept only nonnegative values. The design-time values for R listed above contain no decreasing pair, so negative values are not expected; the rule is stated so that the artifact is exact if one occurs. The fractional part of the slope is not reported because it is not computable in bounded integer arithmetic; readers can compute it from the exact pair. Ratios, exponents, mantissas, and brackets are descriptive numbers over a finite range of n from 3 to 14; no label such as polynomial or exponential is attached to them, and no claim is made that this range distinguishes growth classes.

## Artifact layout

The artifact is one JSON document with `schema_version`, `experiment` (`program-enumeration-v1`), `status`, `protocol_sha256`, the reference declarations above, `budget` (the formula and the per-n values of B(n)), `sizes` (an array of `{size, count, sha256}` for sizes 1 to 15 in order), `observations` (below), `results` (one object per n in ascending order), and the reference summary. Two implementations following this section produce value-identical artifacts.

**Observations.** `observations` is one flat array, ordered by n ascending and within n by kind in the order given here, of rows each carrying `kind` and `n`. Kind `case`: the corpus rows of T_n then H_n in corpus order with the per-case fields above and `corpus` (`gate` or `held_out`). Kind `reference-program`, two rows, `which` `r` then `twin`: `id`, `text`, `work` (an array over the formulas of T_n in corpus order of full `Work` vectors in the `Work::json` field order), `held_out_work` (the same over H_n), `worst: {formula, work_units, work}` and `worst_without_rule_attempts: {formula, work_units}` over the known-labelled formulas of T_n, `held_out_worst: {formula, work_units}`, `total_work_units`, `frames` (an array over the structured formulas of T_n, then `cube-low` of H_n where present, of `{formula, search_nodes}`; the `cube-low` entry is read from `held_out_work`), `random_max_frames: {formula, search_nodes}` over all 18 random cases, and `family_worst` (an object from family name to the family maximum used by the successive ratios). ## Acceptance and reporting

The run is valid only if the enumerator's counts match the recurrence for every size 1 to 15; R is enumerated exactly once, at size 15; every reference label passes its check (truth-table coverage of 2^n assignments and agreement with construction labels, a construction label's structural check and, for SAT, a verified model, a verified model or an accepted LRAT proof for oracle cases) or the case is labelled unknown by a conflict limit or a proof over the cap; every DIMACS digest of an oracle case has exactly one entry in the answer file and every entry is used; where the pinned binary is present, the regenerated answer file is byte-identical to the committed one; the validity precondition holds for R and the twin; and replay completes within the runtime bound below. A failure of any of these stops the run without a result, and a mismatch between an artifact and the checks below is repaired transparently before publication, preserving the frozen design. Language, order, bound, corpus, or budget changes prompted by observed results require a new protocol identity.

**Implementation checks.** The following are not registered claims. They are consequences of the fixed corpus, the charge table, the budget argument, and the pre-freeze design-time model, so they test the interpreter and the labels, not the programs. A violation is an implementation defect: it is reviewed before publication, the repair preserves the frozen design, the run is redone when the repair changes any value, and the finding with its resolution is recorded in the results note `experiments/program-enumeration.md` beside the artifact. Where the review finds the protocol's own argument wrong rather than the implementation, the frozen text is preserved, the observed values stand in the published artifact, and the finding is recorded the same way.

- **Counts and identity.** The enumerator produces exactly the stated counts for sizes 1 to 15 without duplicates, R appears exactly once, at size 15, and the per-size digests are reproduced. Failure stops the run.
- **Validity precondition.** R and the twin complete every known-labelled formula of T_n and H_n within B(n)/4 and agree with every known label there (this tests the interpreter and the labels, as the focused tests do at 2 and 3 variables), so R and the twin are in every C_n and bounded-tested at every n; and every reference label passes its check. Failure stops the run without a result; an oracle unknown is not a failure.
- **Label counts.** The known-label counts per n equal the expected values above, except that an oracle unknown at n = 13 or 14 reduces the known count of its corpus by one without a disagreement.
- **Frame predictions.** R's and the twin's `search_nodes` on every structured formula with a closed form above equal that closed form, and their maxima over all 18 random cases of T_n, regardless of label, equal the listed values and are first attained on the listed formulas.
- **Cost table.** R's worst-case `work_units` over all 18 random cases of T_n, regardless of label, is strictly increasing in n from 3 to 14, as the design-time values above are; the twin's worst case over T_3 is 887, and over the same 18 random cases it equals R's, on the same first attaining formula, at every n from 4 to 14. In addition, every survivor whose worst-case ratio to R at n is below its ratio at n−1 is flagged in `ratio_decrease` with both exact pairs, compared by cross-multiplication in checked `u128`, never by their thousandths strings; the flag is a descriptive report, and the design-time model already predicts it for the twin between n = 3 and n = 4 (887/864 then 1687/1687). Successive ratios and slope brackets are reported as descriptive numbers under the rule above; no finite range distinguishes growth classes, and no growth label is attached.
- **No unknowns.** U_n is empty at every n and no survivor is held-out unknown, as the budget argument proves. An `unknown` outcome in the run is a finding about the interpreter, the charge table, or the argument, handled exactly as this paragraph's opening states; the run stays valid, the program is reported in U_n or as held-out unknown by the staging rules, and no claim is affected except that claim 1 is not established while U_n is nonempty for some n.

Keep every corpus formula and label, every unknown case with its reason, every listed survivor with its rank, text, per-formula outcome and events on T_n and H_n as laid out above, every listed member of U_n with its size, rank, and stopping formula, and every refuted program's refuting formula and outcome in aggregate by size, by outcome kind, and by formula. Report per-n results before any aggregate. Report the absence of survivors at a size or n, and every unknown, with the same completeness as their presence.

## Replay and runtime

Replay is deterministic and single-threaded: enumeration in size-then-rank order, stages in n order, formulas in corpus order. The expected replay is about 60 seconds in release mode on a laptop, within a range of 35 to 85 seconds, against the bound of 120 seconds. The basis uses the pre-freeze measurements of about 10^8 declared events per second and 1 to 3 microseconds per program for generation, formatting, and hashing (a 15-node canonical text is at most 140 bytes, hashed at several hundred MB/s, inside that allowance). Stage 3 dominates: 24,953,959 programs at the measured 37 events per program are about 0.92 × 10^9 events, about 9 seconds, plus 25 to 75 seconds of generation, formatting, and hashing; stages 4 to 14 evaluate only survivors, and with the expected |C_3| of 2, whose cost the design-time model puts near 10^5 events per formula, they need under 10^7 events for two programs on 35 formulas per n (36 at n = 6 and 12); the truth-table reference at n ≤ 12, the DPLL reference point, the construction checks, and the verification of 60 oracle entries add under 10^8 events, about 1 second. The range is 9 + 25 + 1 to 9 + 75 + 1 seconds. The 37 events per program was measured through size 12 only; an average of 100 to 200 events per program over all sizes is not part of the range but a margin, and its upper end (about 5 × 10^9 events, 50 seconds of evaluation) would put the replay at 76 to 126 seconds, so a measured average near 200 at sizes 13 to 15 makes the remedy below apply. The only other unknown in this basis is |C_3|. The proved fallback is the budget argument: every evaluation at stage n and on H_n is bounded by (2^(n+1) − 1) · 15 · (3L + 2C + 3) + C + L + 1 events with C = 6n and L = 17n, so stages 4 to 14 together are bounded by |C_3| · Σ_{n=4}^{14} (|T_n| + |H_n|) · that bound, which is |C_3| · 28,370,662,686 events, about 284 seconds per member of C_3 at 10^8 events per second. The bound is loose by a factor of about 91,654 for R, whose design-time worst cases sum to 309,540 events over n = 4 to 14 (28,370,662,686 / 309,540); a large |C_3| is the case in which the 120 second rule would bite, and the remedy then applies. An artifact whose replay exceeds 120 seconds is not admitted; since S_max cannot drop below the size of R, the remedy is an implementation optimization that changes no observable value, or a smaller grammar under a new protocol identity, never an ad hoc skip. The gate prints the replay's wall seconds and warns above 120 s; no wall-clock figure enters the artifact.

## Focused correctness tests

The parser round-trips every construct, rejects `unit` and `lit` outside their binders, wrong types, wrong arities, and nesting beyond 64 (a parser test; enumerated programs have depth at most 15), and accepts `self` at any depth. The scoping test evaluates `(with-literal input (with-literal (assign input lit) (empty? input) (empty? input)) false)` on the formula `((1))`, requiring `false`: the inner binder's argument evaluates to the empty formula and takes the else branch, but `input` there is still the frame formula `((1))`, which is not empty (a rebinding reading would answer `true`); and it evaluates `(with-literal input (with-literal (assign input lit) (self (assign input lit)) true) false)` on `((1) (2))`, requiring `true`: the inner binder's argument is `((2))`, so the inner `lit` is 2, and the `self` argument `(assign input lit)` is `((1))` from the frame formula, whose next frame answers `true`; a rebinding reading would recurse on the empty formula and answer `false`. The enumerator's counts for sizes 1 to 12 match the recurrence in `cargo test` and no size up to 12 contains a duplicate canonical text, and the run's acceptance check covers sizes 13 to 15; its programs of sizes 1 and 2 are exactly `true`, `false`, `(empty? input)`, `(conflict? input)`, `(self input)` in that order; every enumerated program parses and re-serializes to itself; R has size 15. R, the twin, and the 21-node DPLL program agree with the truth table on every subset of the nine canonical width-at-most-two clauses on two variables (the empty clause, `(1)`, `(-1)`, `(2)`, `(-2)`, `(1 2)`, `(1 -2)`, `(-1 2)`, `(-1 -2)`, listed here so the test depends on no private constant) and on every subset of the eight width-3 clauses on three variables; R's `search_nodes` on the chains, the parity cycles, the cubes, and the two pigeonhole formulas equal the closed forms above; `(self input)` is `invalid` on every formula; `(with-literal input (self (assign input lit)) (empty? input))` is refuted on T_3 and the test records the first refuting mask it observes rather than asserting one; `(with-literal input true (empty? input))` is refuted by the full cube. The two scalar reads with no `lib.rs` analogue are pinned exactly: `(if true true false)` on the empty formula at n = 0 charges `rule_attempts` 3, `literal_reads` 1 (the Boolean), `literal_writes` 1 (the validation count write), and nothing else, `work_units` 5; `(self input)` on `((1))` at n = 1 charges `clause_reads` 1, `literal_reads` 3 (the validation read and the two count reads), `literal_writes` 1, `formula_checks` 1, `rule_attempts` 2, `search_nodes` 1, `work_units` 9, and stops `invalid`. The charge deltas against `lib.rs` are exact: on every subset of the nine canonical two-variable clauses and every subset of the eight cube clauses (768 formulas), the test evaluates formula-level `(with-unit input (assign input unit))` through this language's formula evaluator and `UNIT_RULE`, `(with-unit input (rewrite input unit true true))`, through `lib.rs`, both totals including case validation, on a formula with C clauses and L literal occurrences, and requires, when the formula has a singleton clause, `lib.rs` `clause_reads` equal to this language's `clause_reads` + 2C, `literal_reads` + 2L − 1, `clause_writes` + 2C, and `literal_writes` + 2L − 4, with `formula_checks` = 2, `rule_attempts` = 5, and `search_nodes` = 0 on this side; and, when it has none, `clause_reads` + C, `literal_reads` + L, `clause_writes` + C, and `literal_writes` + L − 1, with `formula_checks` = 2 and `rule_attempts` = 2. ## Limitations

The experiment is finite: 24,953,959 programs of one grammar on about two dozen formulas at each of 12 values of n. A bound of 15 nodes admits R and its twin and nothing larger: it excludes unit propagation at 19 and 21 nodes, every one-node variant of R at 16, and every clause-learning procedure; the fixed choice primitives exclude every branching heuristic other than first literal and first unit. The bound was set by the replay-time rule, not by the question, and the second curve is accordingly a table of R-like costs whose analytic values are stated above. Survivors are correct on a corpus; held-out agreement raises them only to bounded-tested. The staged definition measures uniform survivors; the non-uniform minimum at n ≥ 4 is not measured. The chains and parity cycles are in P and measure fallback semantics; the random cases at n = 3 are cube multisets. Labels at n = 13 and 14 rest on a construction with a cited proof and a structural check, or on a checked model or a checked LRAT proof from one pinned solver; a conflict-limit exhaustion is an unknown, not a label. Declared events are operational, not a bit-cost model, and include an interpretive rule attempt per node. Universal search is complete only in the limit of unbounded size, and its constants are what this experiment measures within one grammar: the absence of a small fast program below the bound says nothing about programs above it, other grammars, or all algorithms, and no P versus NP conclusion follows in either direction.
