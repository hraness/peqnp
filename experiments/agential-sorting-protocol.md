## Question and distinct outputs

Zhang, Goldstein, and Levin report that when the three classical sorts are re-implemented as policies executed by each array element with a local view, the arrays still sort, tolerate "frozen" elements better than the top-down versions, temporarily reduce sortedness to get around a defect, and, when two policies are mixed in one array, transiently cluster by policy although no policy reads the policy of any cell. They ran threads; their timing is not reproducible. Part A asks whether those three findings survive a fully deterministic sequential semantics with every comparison and write charged in this laboratory's event model. Part B asks what the same architecture does when the cells are Boolean variables of a random 3-CNF, the local view is the clauses a variable occurs in, and the move is a flip: it measures a family of collective local-search heuristics and their chimeric mixes under damage.

Three statements bound the interpretation and are repeated in the artifact's report header:

1. Every "competency" measured here is a consequence of the code specified below. Delayed gratification, aggregation, and error tolerance are names for numeric functionals of trajectories that the specified rules produce; nothing is explained by them, and the protocol records the code paths that produce them where a proof is short.
2. Part B measures collective search heuristics on satisfiable random formulas of at most 24 variables. Success rates and event counts describe those heuristics on that finite corpus.
3. Nothing in this experiment bears on worst-case complexity or on P versus NP. Local search never refutes: a `stalled` or `budget` outcome is a definite non-success of that heuristic inside its fixed budget and says nothing about the formula's satisfiability, which only the reference decides; a finite experiment proves no asymptotic bound.

Distinct outputs: Part A reports, per algorithm and damage configuration, the final monotonicity error, the sortedness trajectory functionals, and, for chimeras, the aggregation trajectory functionals. Part B reports, per algotype configuration and damage level, success counts over labelled-satisfiable cases, budget and stall counts, events per solved case by category, the unsatisfied-clause trajectory functionals, and per-algotype flip shares. Every registered claim below is an uncertain prediction with a numeric falsification criterion on the finite corpus; facts that follow from the rules and the fixed corpus are implementation checks in the acceptance section, and every other number is descriptive.

## Shared conventions

**Events.** Every charge names one category: formula check, clause or container read, clause or container write, literal or scalar read, literal or scalar write, assignment, pair check, rule attempt, search node. No search nodes occur in this experiment; the category is reported as zero. Probe work (metric evaluation between actions) is outside every arm and is not charged, exactly as the paper's Probe object observes without acting; the probe's incremental updates are described so that the artifact's trajectory digests are reproducible.

**Budget stop.** One rule for both parts and for traditional runs. Every charged event is ticked on the run's meter before the operation it names; the meter refuses the first event that would carry the count past the budget, and the run ends at that moment, mid-round, mid-activation, or mid-loop, with outcome `budget`. The refused event is not counted, so the recorded event total of a `budget` run equals the budget exactly and its category counters sum to the budget. A compound write, meaning a swap's two scalar writes or a flip's assignment together with its per-occurrence scalar writes, charges all of its events before applying any of them, so a refusal inside a compound write leaves the array or assignment unchanged and the array is always a permutation of its input; a refusal anywhere else leaves every earlier applied write in place. The probe records a trajectory entry only after a swap or flip has been applied, so a refused compound write contributes no entry; the performed-swap and flip counters count applied writes only. This is the crate's `Meter::tick` semantics (`src/transfer.rs`), stated so that the event totals, the last trajectory entry, and the trajectory digest of every `budget` run are fixed by the text.

**Rounds counter.** `rounds` counts every round started, including the round in which the run ended: the quiescent round of a `quiescent` run, round `R_cap` of a `capped` run, the round in which the refusal occurred for a `budget` run, and the round in which `U` reached zero for a `solved` run. For traditional runs `rounds` is the number of outer-loop iterations started: the number of the pass in which traditional bubble ended, and 99 for a `finished` traditional insertion or selection run, whose outer loops (`i = 1..99` and `i = 0..98`) each start 99 iterations. A Part B arm that ends before round 1 (initially satisfied, below) reports `rounds = 0`.

**Numbers.** Every non-integer quantity is a `RatioPair` `{numerator, denominator, thousandths}` computed by `ratio_pair`, or `null` when the denominator is zero. `ratio_pair` also returns `null` when `numerator * 1000 + denominator / 2` overflows a 64-bit unsigned integer (`src/oracle.rs`); the largest numerator this experiment can produce is an event sum below the Part A hard bound of 8.9 x 10^10, and 8.9 x 10^13 is below 2^64 (about 1.8 x 10^19), so on this corpus a `null` means a zero denominator and nothing else. Every signed quantity here (`d(s)`, `d~(s)`, `e(s)`, and differences of `D` sums) is an integer, so the policy's signed form `SignedRatioPair` is not needed and is not used. No floating point reaches the artifact. `RatioPair` bounds and orderings in claims are decided by cross-multiplication of the integer pairs, never by the thousandths string. Successive ratios, where reported, are `RatioPair`s with the finite-range caveat and carry no growth-class label; no log-log slope is reported because no family here spans more than a factor of two in size.

**Generator.** All random draws use the xorshift64 transition `x ^= x << 13; x ^= x >> 7; x ^= x << 17` on a 64-bit unsigned state with truncating shifts, `next()` returning the new state. Initial states are stated per part. A state of zero never arises for the stated seeds; the implementation rejects it as malformed input.

**Budget and cap outcomes.** A run that exhausts its event budget has outcome `budget`; a Part A run that completes round `R_cap` without quiescence has outcome `capped`. Both are reported in every table with their counters and digests. What they mean for a claim is stated per part: in Part A, `capped` and `budget` runs count as not sorted for the final-state claims A1 and A4 and enter the trajectory-functional claims A3 and A5 with the functionals of their recorded trajectory, counted and reported beside each sum; in Part B, a `budget` outcome is a definite non-success of that arm inside its fixed budget, counted as not solved in every success count, never an unknown about the case. The only unknown in this experiment is an oracle `unknown` label (conflict limit or proof over cap): it keeps the case in the artifact with its arm results, excludes the case from every comparison, and is never re-drawn. The earlier protocols' rule that comparisons count only cases where all compared arms completed (`experiments/indexed-transfer-protocol.md`, `experiments/fragment-interface-protocol.md`, `experiments/extraction-cost-protocol.md`) concerns deciding arms; every Part B arm here completes with `solved`, `stalled`, or `budget`, and all three are completions of a fixed-budget heuristic.

## Part A: cells and arrays

An array has `L = 100` positions `0..L-1`, each holding one cell. A cell is a record `(value, algotype, direction, frozen, target, stamp, label)`. `value` is an integer; `algotype` is one of `bubble`, `insertion`, `selection`; `direction` is `+1` (increasing) or `-1` (decreasing); `frozen` is `none`, `movable`, or `immovable`; `target` is an integer used by `selection` cells, initially 0; `stamp` is the round number of the cell's last activation, initially 0; `label` is a bit used by chimeric runs and inert otherwise. The comparison `before(a, b)` means `a < b` when the acting cell's direction is `+1` and `a > b` when it is `-1`; it is one pair check, and it is strict, so equal values never move.

**Two value families.** `unique`: the values are a permutation of `1..100`. `duplicate`: the values are a permutation of the multiset with each of `1..10` occurring ten times. For base seed `s` and family index `f` (`1` unique, `2` duplicate) the generator state is `s XOR (100 << 32) XOR (f << 40)`. Draw order, one generator per `(s, f)`: (1) the identity arrangement (unique: `1..100` in order; duplicate: value `1 + i div 10` at position `i`) is shuffled by Fisher-Yates: for `i` from `99` down to `1`, `j = next() mod (i + 1)`, swap positions `i` and `j`; (2) three frozen positions: repeat `next() mod 100`, rejecting a position already drawn, until three are accepted, in draw order; (3) one hundred label draws, `label_i = next() AND 1` for position `i` from `0` to `99`. Every run on `(s, f)` derives from these draws: a run with `k` frozen cells freezes the cells initially at the first `k` drawn positions (so the frozen sets are nested in `k`), all with the run's frozen kind; the labels are attached to the cells at those initial positions and travel with the cells.

**Setup is uncharged.** Building the initial array from the draws, including the initial `value`, `algotype`, `direction`, `frozen`, `target`, `stamp`, and `label` of every cell, charges nothing; a run's charged events begin with its first visit (cell-view) or its first outer-loop iteration (traditional), so every run's setup total is zero and all runs on one `(s, f)` start from the same charged count.

**Frozen cells.** A frozen cell of either kind never initiates a move. A `movable` frozen cell may be moved by another cell's swap; an `immovable` frozen cell may not. Frozen status is a property of the cell, not of the position.

## Part A: activation and rounds

A cell-view run proceeds in rounds `r = 1, 2, ...`. In round `r`, positions `p = 0..L-1` are **visited** in ascending order, once each. A visit charges one scalar read of the `stamp` of the cell at `p`; if it equals `r`, the visit ends and the cell is not activated. Otherwise the visit becomes the cell's **activation**: one scalar write sets `stamp = r`; one scalar read of `frozen`; if frozen, the activation ends. Otherwise one rule attempt, then the algotype rule below. A cell's own `value` is held by the cell and its use is not charged; every other field read is charged as listed. Every cell is activated at most once per round. This is the sequential stand-in for the paper's concurrently running threads: one action per activation, in ascending position order at the time the scan reaches it. A cell that moves right lands on a position the scan has not reached and is skipped there by its stamp; a cell displaced left by a swap lands on a position the scan has passed and does not act this round, whether it has already acted (`stamp = r`) or not (`stamp < r`); in the latter case it loses its activation for the round, which is intended: its stamp is never read at the passed position, so the invariant is that every cell acts at most once per round, not that every cell acts. A swap partner that lands on `p` itself, which is the bubble left-swap and insertion partner arriving from `p - 1`, the bubble right-swap partner arriving from `p + 1`, and the selection partner arriving from `t`, is not activated in that visit: the visit to `p` is one activation, of the cell that occupied `p` when the scan reached it, and the scan then moves to `p + 1`.

**Swap attempt** by an initiator at position `i` with a partner at position `j`: one scalar read of the partner's `frozen`; if it is `immovable`, the swap is denied and counted; otherwise two scalar writes are charged and then the two array cells are exchanged (the compound-write rule of the budget stop), the swap is counted, and the probe records. The initiator is never frozen at this point.

**Bubble rule** for the cell at `p` with value `v`: if `p > 0`, one scalar read of the value at `p - 1` and one pair check `before(v, left)`; if true, attempt a swap with `p - 1`; if that swap is performed, the activation ends. If the left check was false, or the swap was denied, and `p < L - 1`: one scalar read of the value at `p + 1` and one pair check `before(right, v)`; if true, attempt a swap with `p + 1`. The activation ends.

**Insertion rule** for the cell at `p`: if `p = 0`, the activation ends. Otherwise one scalar read of the value at `p - 1`; then for `q` from `p - 1` down to `1`: one scalar read of the value at `q - 1` and one pair check `before(value at q, value at q - 1)`; if true, the prefix is unsorted and the activation ends. If the scan reaches `q = 1` without a violation (the prefix `0..p-1` is sorted in the cell's direction), one pair check `before(v, value at p - 1)`; if true, attempt a swap with `p - 1`. The scan runs right to left and stops at the first violation; it decides the same predicate as reading the whole prefix, at lower cost.

**Selection rule** for the cell at `p`: one scalar read of `target`, call it `t`. If `t > p`, one scalar write sets `target = p` (a state change) and the activation ends. If `t = p`, the activation ends. Otherwise one scalar read of the value at `t` and one pair check `before(v, value at t)`; if true, attempt a swap with `t` (the partner moves to `p`); if the swap is performed, the activation ends; if it is denied, one scalar write sets `target = t + 1`. If the check is false, one scalar write sets `target = t + 1`. Targets never decrease except by the `t > p` clamp, which can occur only in chimeric or cross-purpose runs where another algotype displaces a cell leftward; in a pure selection run `target <= position` is invariant.

**State change** means a performed swap or a `target` write. A round with no state change is **quiescent** and ends the run with outcome `quiescent`. A run also ends with outcome `capped` when round `R_cap` completes without quiescence, and with outcome `budget` under the budget-stop rule of the shared conventions, at the first refused event. `R_cap = 5000` for the `pure` and `chimera` families. Derivation: every swap of an adjacent-swap algotype (bubble, insertion) exchanges a strictly inverted adjacent pair and removes exactly one inversion, and neither algotype writes a target, so a pure `cv-bubble`, pure `cv-insertion`, or `bubble+insertion` run performs at most `I` swaps, `I` the initial inversion count (at most 4,950 for `unique`, 4,500 for `duplicate`), and every round but the quiescent one performs a swap, so its rounds are at most 4,951 and 5,000 is above that bound; `capped` cannot occur in those runs (an implementation check). In runs with selection cells every performed swap still removes at least one inversion (it exchanges an inverted pair), but target writes are also state changes: in a pure `cv-selection` run each cell's target is nondecreasing and at most 99, so the rules bound rounds by 4,950 swap rounds plus 9,900 target-write rounds plus one, 14,851, and in mixes with selection cells the clamp voids that count; for selection-containing runs 5,000 is therefore this protocol's choice, not a rule bound, and `capped` is a possible outcome for them. `R_cap = 1000` for `cross`, where the cap is the design: it is this protocol's choice of ten times `L`, ten full traversals of the array by an adjacent-swap cell; the design-time run of `bubble(-1)+selection(+1)` on seed 7001 reached it. The budget is 40,000,000 events for every family, `cross` included, under the same budget-stop rule; a round charges at most 10,606 events on any array (derived in the budgets section), so a `cross` run charges at most 1,000 x 10,606 = 10,606,000 events and a `budget` outcome cannot occur there (an implementation check).

## Part A: traditional versions

The top-down algorithms run on the same cell records under the same frozen rules with the initiator and partner named per algorithm. A traditional swap attempt charges one scalar read of the initiator's `frozen`; if it is `movable` or `immovable`, the swap is denied and counted with no further read; otherwise one scalar read of the partner's `frozen`; if it is `immovable`, the swap is denied and counted; otherwise two scalar writes are charged and the cells are exchanged, the swap is counted, and the probe records. A denied swap costs one or two scalar reads and nothing else, and the algorithm continues its loop as if nothing happened. Each outer-loop iteration charges one rule attempt. Direction is `+1`.

- **Traditional bubble:** for pass `i` from `1` to `L - 1`: for `j` from `0` to `L - 1 - i`: two scalar reads and one pair check `value(j + 1) < value(j)`; if true, attempt a swap with initiator at `j` (the larger element moving right) and partner at `j + 1`. A pass that performs no swap ends the run.
- **Traditional insertion:** for `i` from `1` to `L - 1`: `j = i`; while `j > 0`: two scalar reads and one pair check `value(j) < value(j - 1)`; if false, stop; if true, attempt a swap with initiator at `j` (the element being inserted) and partner at `j - 1`; if denied, stop; if performed, `j = j - 1`.
- **Traditional selection:** for `i` from `0` to `L - 2`: `mn = i`; for `j` from `i + 1` to `L - 1`: two scalar reads and one pair check `value(j) < value(mn)`; if true, one scalar write `mn = j`. If `mn != i`, attempt a swap with initiator at `mn` (the minimum moving left) and partner at `i`.

Traditional runs end with outcome `finished`, or `budget` under the same 40,000,000-event budget and the same budget-stop rule; their rule bound of 35,244 events (budgets section) lies below the budget, so `budget` cannot occur (an implementation check).

## Part A: metrics

All metrics are evaluated in the increasing direction regardless of any cell's direction.

- **Monotonicity error** `E`: the number of positions `i` in `1..L-1` with `value(i - 1) > value(i)`. An integer.
- **Sortedness** `S = (L - E) / L`, a `RatioPair`. `S = 1` iff the array is nondecreasing. The artifact stores `E` per run; `S` is recovered as `ratio_pair(L - E, L)` and is stored only in the claim tables.
- **Trajectory.** The probe records `c_0 = L - E` for the initial array and `c_j = L - E` after the `j`-th performed swap. It updates `E` incrementally by re-examining the at most four adjacent pairs touched by the swap; the artifact stores the SHA-256 of the trajectory serialized as decimal integers, each followed by one `\n` including the last, beginning with `c_0`, plus its length, so a replay verifies the whole trajectory without storing it.
- **Dips and delayed gratification.** Scan the trajectory left to right from index `i = 0`. If `c_{i+1} < c_i`, a dip begins at peak `c_i`: advance `j` from `i + 1` while `j < last` and `c_{j+1} <= c_j`; the trough is `c_j`. Then advance `k` from `j` while `k < last` and `c_{k+1} >= c_k`; the recovery ends at `c_k`. The dip's drop is `c_i - c_j` and its rise is `c_k - c_j` (zero when the trajectory ends in the drop). Resume the scan at `k`. If `c_{i+1} >= c_i`, advance `i`. Per run: `D` = sum of drops (integer, "sortedness surrendered"), `X` = sum of rises, `N_dips`, and `R = X / D` as a `RatioPair` or `null`; the artifact stores `D`, `X`, and `N_dips` per run and `R` in the claim tables, where it is recovered as `ratio_pair(X, D)`. The paper's Figure 6D legend (pp. 29 to 30 of arXiv:2401.05375v1; the Methods definition on p. 9 uses the same two consecutive sortedness changes) defines its Delayed Gratification index as the total consecutive increase of sortedness after the drop (`x`) over the total consecutive decrease before it (`y`), which is this protocol's per-dip `X / D`; what the paper leaves unstated is whether its reported figures aggregate per dip or over the summed totals, so `D`, `X`, and `R` as defined here, with `R` on the summed totals, are this protocol's definitions.
- **Aggregation** `A`: the number of adjacent position pairs `(i, i + 1)` whose cells carry the same label, an integer in `0..L-1`, reported over `L - 1` as a `RatioPair` in the claim tables. Its expectation under independent uniform labels is `(L - 1) / 2`, the paper's 0.5 baseline (Results pp. 13 to 14 of arXiv:2401.05375v1: 0.5 at the start and at the end of every chimeric run; Figure 8 legend, pp. 33 to 34: 50% chance). The paper's Methods (p. 9) define aggregation per cell as the percentage of cells whose directly adjacent neighbours are all of the same algotype, whose random baseline would be 0.25; the Figure 8 legend (pp. 33 to 34) defines it as the probability that a cell's neighbour is of the same type, which with the reported 0.5 baseline is the per-pair form used here. The probe records `A_j` alongside `c_j`; per run: `A_peak = max_j A_j` over `j` from 0, with the first index attaining it, and `A_final`. For the runs named in the artifact layout section the artifact also stores the run's **running-maximum profile** of `A`: the list of `(j, A_j)` at which the running maximum first attains each new value, at most `L` entries because `A` takes at most `L` values, whose last entry is `A_peak` with its index; the length-matched peak `A_peak(l)` over the prefix `0..l-1` is the value of the last profile entry with `j < l`, recoverable from the artifact for any `l`. Every run records `A_peak`, its index, and `A_final`, including pure runs, whose labels are inert; the pure run of the same `(s, f)` is the same-algorithm control for a chimera.

## Part A: corpus and records

Enumerate families in this order, then seeds, then the listed parameters in the displayed order.

- **`pure`**, 2,016 runs: family `f` in `unique, duplicate`; algorithm in `cv-bubble, cv-insertion, cv-selection, trad-bubble, trad-insertion, trad-selection`; damage configuration in `k = 0` and `(k, kind)` for `k` in `1, 2, 3` and `kind` in `movable, immovable` (seven configurations); 24 seeds. All cells share the algorithm; labels are inert.
- **`chimera`**, 144 runs: `f` in `unique, duplicate`; mix in `bubble+insertion, bubble+selection, insertion+selection`; `k = 0`; 24 seeds. A cell with label 0 runs the first-named algotype and a cell with label 1 the second; both directions `+1`.
- **`cross`**, 72 runs: `f = unique`; mix and directions in `bubble(-1)+selection(+1)`, `bubble(+1)+insertion(-1)`, `selection(-1)+insertion(+1)`, the three opposite-direction pairings of the paper's Figure 9 (legend p. 35 of arXiv:2401.05375v1, which reports the winning order bubble, selection, insertion and does not state which algotype of a pair sorted in which direction; the direction assignment per pairing is this protocol's); `k = 0`; 24 seeds; `R_cap = 1000`; a cell with label 0 runs the first-named algotype in its stated direction and a cell with label 1 the second, as in `chimera`. The run's final state is the state at quiescence or at the cap, and claims about it are claims about that state, not about any limit.

Per run the artifact records, in the compact form of the artifact layout section: outcome, `rounds`, performed and denied swaps, events (total and the four categories Part A charges: scalar reads, scalar writes, pair checks, rule attempts), final `E`, `D`, `X`, `N_dips`, `A_peak` with its index, `A_final`, trajectory length and digest, and, for the runs the layout section names, the running-maximum profile of `A`. The run's identity (family, value family, algorithm or mix, damage configuration, seed) is its position in the enumeration above, which the layout section fixes as nested tables.

## Part A: registered claims

Outcomes other than `quiescent` and `finished` are handled by two rules. For the final-state claims A1 and A4, a run with outcome `capped` or `budget` counts as not sorted: it cannot contribute to `Z`, to the 22-of-24 count, or to a mean-sortedness bound as a sorted run, and its final `E` enters every mean; the claim is decided over all 24 seeds regardless. For the trajectory-functional claims A3 and A5, a run with outcome `capped` or `budget` enters with the `A_peak`, profile, and `A_final` of its recorded trajectory, which are well defined on a truncated trajectory, and the number of such runs entering each sum is reported beside it; the length confound this adds is the one A3 already handles by `d~(s)`. The runs of claim A2 are `cv-bubble` runs, which cannot be `capped` or `budget` (rounds at most 4,951 and events at most 6,000,000; an implementation check), so A2 is always decided. Descriptive `D`-based functionals of a `capped` or `budget` run are reported with the flag `truncated`. The `cross` family is the design: A4's cross-purpose bounds are about the state at quiescence or at the cap.

**A1, error tolerance with movable frozen cells.** Pooled over `k` in `1, 2, 3`, kind `movable`, family `unique`, 72 runs per algorithm: let `Z(alg)` be the number of runs with final `E = 0` and `T(alg)` the sum of final `E` over the 72 runs. Claims: `Z(cv-bubble) >= Z(trad-bubble) + 24`; `Z(cv-insertion) >= Z(trad-insertion) + 6`; and `T(cv-insertion) <= T(trad-insertion)`; each falsified separately when its inequality fails. The paper reports mean final `E` of 0, 0.8, and 2.64 for cell-view bubble at 1, 2, and 3 movable frozen cells and the movable-cell error-tolerance order bubble, insertion, selection (Results p. 11 and Figure 5B legend p. 27 of arXiv:2401.05375v1). Derivation of the margins: under the bubble rule, whose swaps are adjacent, two movable frozen cells can never exchange places (a selection swap, by contrast, carries a movable partner across every cell between `t` and `p`), so a cell-view bubble run with an inverted pair of frozen cells ends with `E >= 1`; the fraction of frozen sets with no inverted pair is `1, 1/2, 1/6` for `k = 1, 2, 3` in expectation over the shuffle, so if nothing else blocks, the expected `Z(cv-bubble)` is `24 + 12 + 4 = 40`; a traditional bubble run keeps every smaller element that starts right of a frozen cell on its right, which is rare to be harmless; 24 is sixty percent of the expected count. For insertion the structure favors neither version and predicts that both `Z` counts are small: with one movable frozen cell, either version sorts iff the frozen cell's value exceeds every value initially to its left. In the cell-view version a frozen cell smaller than some value to its left keeps the prefix ending at it unsorted forever, so no cell to its right ever passes the prefix check and the run deadlocks early with a large `E`; in the traditional version that one cell's insertion is denied, the larger values to its left are never passed, and every other element is inserted, so the run finishes with a small `E`. Both `Z` counts are therefore expected near one to three of 72, the margin of 6 (one twelfth) registers the paper's reported direction with no mechanism behind it and is expected to be falsified, and the `T` claim registers the same direction on the mean, where the deadlock is visible as a large cell-view `E` against a small traditional one. A falsification of the two insertion clauses is the expected replication failure of the paper's direction under these rules, not a harness defect, and is reported as such. Per-`k` counts, mean final `E` as a `RatioPair`, and the immovable kind are reported descriptively for all six algorithms.

**A2, delayed gratification grows with damage.** Family `unique`, `cv-bubble`, kind `movable`: `D_k` is the sum of `D` over the 24 seeds at damage `k`. Claim: `D_0 < D_1 < D_2 < D_3`, falsified when any inequality fails (the paper reports mean delayed gratification of 0.24, 0.29, 0.32, and 0.37 for cell-view bubble at 0 to 3 frozen cells, Results p. 12 of arXiv:2401.05375v1). Secondary registered claim: the same for kind `immovable`. Descriptive: `D` per performed swap as a `RatioPair`, `R`, `N_dips`, and the same functionals for `cv-insertion`, `cv-selection`, and the traditional versions, including the difference between cell-view and traditional `D` sums that the paper reports as a Z-test. Note that under per-swap sampling an adjacent swap can raise `E` by creating violations at its flanks, so `D_0 > 0` is expected; the claim is about growth in `k`.

**A3, chimeric aggregation exceeds the same-algorithm control.** Family `unique`, `k = 0`, each of the three mixes: for seed `s`, `d(s) = A_peak(mix, s) - max(A_peak(pure X, s), A_peak(pure Y, s))`, in adjacent-pair units, where `X` and `Y` are the mix's algotypes and the pure runs carry the same labels. Claim: `sum_s d(s) >= 72` (three pairs per seed, about 0.03 in the paper's units, below the paper's smallest reported gap between a chimera's peak and its same-algorithm control, 0.04 for bubble with insertion, 0.65 against 0.61, Results pp. 13 to 14 of arXiv:2401.05375v1), falsified when the sum is smaller, for every mix separately. Stated null for the accompanying statistic: with `P` the number of seeds with `d(s) > 0`, ties counting against, report `P` and the exact upper tail `sum_{i >= P} C(24, i) / 2^24` as a `RatioPair` under the null that the sign of `d` is a fair coin; this is a sign test with an exact integer computation, not a threshold of the claim. The fair-coin null is conservative for the claimed direction: `d(s)` subtracts the larger of two control peaks, so under no effect its sign skews negative, and a large tail is not evidence against aggregation. Limitation of the registered claim: `A_peak` is a maximum over a trajectory, and the mix and its two controls have trajectories of different lengths (on the design-time corpus seed the four relevant lengths differ by up to a factor of 1.8; the figures are in the budgets section), so the expected maximum of a fluctuating `A` differs between arm and control by length alone; the claim is registered on the raw peaks as the paper reports them. Beside it the artifact reports, descriptively, the length-matched difference `d~(s) = A_peak(mix, s; l) - max(A_peak(pure X, s; l), A_peak(pure Y, s; l))` with `l` the minimum of the three trajectory lengths, computed from the stored running-maximum profiles, its sum over seeds, and its sign count with the same exact tail.

**A4, shared-goal chimeras with selection cells sort; cross-purpose chimeras do not.** For `bubble+selection` and `insertion+selection`, where a selection cell's target invariant is broken by adjacent-swap displacement and a stalled inverted pair of selection cells is possible, the claim is: quiescent with `E = 0` in at least 22 of 24 seeds, separately for `unique` and `duplicate`; falsified when fewer, with `capped` and `budget` runs counting against, as stated above. Derivation of 22: the margin allows two failures in 24, one twelfth, the same fraction as A1's insertion margin of 6 in 72, so that a single stalled pair on one seed does not decide the claim while a mechanism that fails one seed in ten does. (`bubble+insertion` always quiesces sorted; that is an implementation check in the acceptance section, not a claim.) For the `cross` family, measured in the increasing direction: for each pairing the mean final sortedness `sum_s (L - E_s) / (24 L)` lies in `[1/4, 17/20]`, and every seed's final `S` lies in `[1/20, 19/20]`; either bound failing falsifies the claim for that pairing; all four bounds are `RatioPair` comparisons by cross-multiplication. Derivation of the brackets: a uniformly random arrangement has expected `E = 99/2`, so `S` near `1/2` is the anchor of a state that neither direction has organised; the sorted state has `S = 1` and the reverse-sorted state `S = 1/100`; the per-seed bracket `[1/20, 19/20]` excludes both (`E` between 5 and 95) on every seed, and the mean bracket `[1/4, 17/20]` is this protocol's choice around the anchor, widened more above `1/2` than below because the paper reports the winning algotype imposing partial order (Figure 9 legend, p. 35), which raises `S` in the increasing direction when the winner sorts increasingly and lowers it toward the anchor otherwise; the paper reports no sortedness values for Figure 9 that the bracket could cite. Whether each cross run reached quiescence before the cap, and its `A_final`, are reported descriptively.

**A5, duplicate values let aggregation persist.** Family `duplicate`, `k = 0`, mixes `bubble+selection` and `insertion+selection`: `e(s) = A_final(mix, s) - max(A_final(pure X, s), A_final(pure Y, s))`. Claim: `sum_s e(s) >= 72` for each of the two mixes; the same sign-test statistic as A3 accompanies it, with the same conservative null. `A_final` is a final value, not a maximum, so the length confound of A3 does not apply to it. `bubble+insertion` on duplicates is reported descriptively.

**Not registered.** The paper's efficiency comparison is reported descriptively as events per run, cell-view against traditional, by category. This harness charges every visit's stamp read and every activation's stamp write and frozen read, so the cell-view versions cost more events than the traditional ones by construction; the number is reported, not claimed.

## Part B: state and local view

A case is a CNF over `n` declared variables with `m` clauses of width three. Per arm run, the state is: `value[1..n]` (Booleans), `frozen[1..n]` (Booleans), `sat_count[c]` for each clause (the number of its literals true under `value`), and occurrence lists `occ[v]` of `(clause index, sign)` in clause order. A variable's **local view** is `occ[v]` together with the current `sat_count` of each listed clause; it sees exactly the clauses its flip can change. The probe maintains `U`, the number of clauses with `sat_count = 0`.

Setup charges, reported as the `setup` phase of the arm: one clause read per clause and one literal read per literal to build the occurrence lists, one container write per list and one scalar write per entry; one container write and `n` scalar writes for `value`; one container write and `n` scalar writes for `frozen`; one clause read, one literal read and one pair check per literal, and one scalar write per clause for `sat_count`. The per-variable algotype table of a mixed arm and the noise-stream state are the arm's configuration, not its state, and are built uncharged; nothing else is charged before the first round, so every arm's setup total on a case is `m + 3m + (n + 3m) + (1 + n) + (1 + n) + (m + 3m + 3m + m) = 15m + 3n + 2` events (1,604 at `n = 24, m = 102`, matching the design-time runs), and two arms on one case have equal setup totals; the artifact therefore stores the setup `Work` once per case, and equality across arms is an implementation check.

**Initially satisfied cases.** After setup the probe evaluates `U_0`. If `U_0 = 0`, the drawn initial assignment already satisfies the formula: the arm ends before round 1 with outcome `solved`, zero flips, zero draws, `rounds = 0`, the trajectory `c_0` alone (length 1), and its setup events as its total; the `check_model` verification of acceptance still runs. Such a case is `solved` by all 28 arms identically (frozen variables and algotypes never act), is counted as solved in every success rate, and is flagged `initially_satisfied` in its case record with its count in every table. Under independent uniform clauses and assignment the probability is `(7/8)^m`, so the expected number of such cases in the corpus is `8 x sum over the twelve cells of (7/8)^m`, about 0.106 (the `m = 36` cell contributes 0.065); zero, one, or two such cases are all consistent with the design.

## Part B: activation, rounds, and algotypes

A round activates variables `1..n` in ascending index, one activation each. Activation of `v`: one scalar read of the frozen flag; frozen ends the activation with no rule attempt. Otherwise one rule attempt, one container read of `occ[v]`, one scalar read of `value[v]`, and, per occurrence, one scalar read of `sat_count[c]` and one pair check of the sign against the value. `breaks` is the number of occurrences whose literal is true with `sat_count = 1`; `makes` is the number whose literal is false with `sat_count = 0`. The decision:

- **greedy (G):** one pair check; flip iff `makes > breaks`.
- **conservative (C):** always two pair checks, no short circuit; flip iff `makes >= 1` and `breaks = 0`.
- **walk (W):** one pair check `makes >= 1`; if false, no flip and no draw; if true, one rule attempt draws `b = next() AND 1` from the arm's noise stream (the stream is configuration, not charged state, so the draw is charged as a rule attempt rather than as a scalar read), then one pair check evaluating the whole disjunction `makes > breaks or b = 1` as a single charged check, and flip iff it holds. The draw is taken whenever `makes >= 1`, whether or not `makes > breaks`, so the noise stream advances identically under any evaluation order. This is a per-variable noisy rule with noise probability `1/2`, inspired by WalkSAT's greedy-or-noisy flip; it is not WalkSAT, which picks one unsatisfied clause per step and flips one of its variables, whereas here every variable with `makes >= 1` flips with probability at least one half in every round, sequentially across the index line. Its noise is a deterministic xorshift stream, so the arm is a fixed function of the case.

A **flip** charges one assignment writing `value[v]` and one scalar write per occurrence adjusting `sat_count[c]`, all charged before any of them is applied (the compound-write rule of the budget stop); the probe then updates `U` and records it. If `U = 0` after a flip the run stops immediately with outcome `solved`. The run stops with outcome `budget` at the first refused event under the budget-stop rule of the shared conventions. A round with no flip and no noise draw ends the run with outcome `stalled`: the state is then a fixed point of every rule, so the failure is definite; a round with a draw but no flip continues. The budget is 200,000 events per arm run including setup; its sizing against the design-time runs is in the budgets section. A pure walk arm at `k = 0` cannot stall, because an unsatisfied clause gives each of its variables `makes >= 1` and some walk cell then draws in every round while `U > 0`; a mixed arm, or any arm with frozen variables, can stall when every variable of every unsatisfied clause is a greedy or conservative cell that declines to flip or a frozen cell that makes no draw. Every arm therefore ends in `solved`, `stalled`, or `budget`; a stall is a definite fixed point of the arm's rules and `budget` is its definite non-success inside the budget, and both count as not solved in every success rate.

Flip conditions nest: conservative implies greedy implies walk. A greedy or conservative flip changes `U` by `breaks - makes < 0`, so in arms without walk cells `U` is strictly decreasing over flips and no dip can occur; `N_dips = 0` in every `G`, `C`, and `GC` run is an implementation check. The local view here covers exactly the clauses a flip touches, so a locally improving move is globally improving; in Part A a bubble cell's view (two neighbors) is narrower than the support of `E` (the flanking pairs), which is the code path behind Part A's dips. Dips in Part B require walk noise.

## Part B: frozen variables and mixes

Damage `k` in `0, 1, 2, 3` freezes the first `k` of the case's three drawn variables at their drawn initial values; a frozen variable is activated but never flips. Configurations are `G, W, C, GW, GC, WC, GWC`; in a two-way mix variable `i` runs the first algotype when `r_i AND 1 = 0` and the second otherwise; in the three-way mix it runs `G` when `r_i mod 3 = 0`, `W` when `r_i mod 3 = 1`, and `C` when `r_i mod 3 = 2`. Arms are the 28 pairs (configuration, `k`) numbered `1..28` with configurations outermost in the order listed and `k` innermost. The noise stream of arm `a` on a case has initial state `case_state XOR (a << 48)` and advances only on walk draws. The fields of these states overlap at bit 48: every density `d` in `300, 400, 426` has bit 8 set, so `d << 40` sets bit 48, which `a << 48` also touches. Distinctness still holds: the 28 noise streams of one case differ from each other because `a` differs, and a noise-stream state equals no case state because XORing a density with 256 gives 44, 144, or 170, none a density, and two densities XOR to 188, 134, or 58, none a multiple of 256.

## Part B: corpus, labels, and records

For base seed `s`, variable count `n`, and density `d` in hundredths (`300, 400, 426`), the case state is `s XOR (n << 32) XOR (d << 40)`. `m = (d * n + 50) div 100`: 36, 48, 51 at `n = 12`; 48, 64, 68 at 16; 60, 80, 85 at 20; 72, 96, 102 at 24. Draw order from one generator: `m` clauses of width three, each by repeating: draw `next() mod n + 1` as a variable, reject it if it already occurs in the clause, and once accepted draw its sign from a fresh draw's low bit, zero meaning positive, stopping after three accepted literals (clauses may repeat); then `n` initial values, `value[i] = (next() AND 1) = 1`; then three distinct frozen variables by `next() mod n + 1` with rejection of repeats; then `n` label draws `r_i = next()`. Enumerate `n` in `12, 16, 20, 24` outermost, then density, then seeds: 96 cases.

Reference model: `truth-table<=12;definition;oracle:cadical-3.0.1+model-check+lrat-check`. The 24 cases at `n = 12` use the truth-table reference of the implication experiment, `implication::reference_wide`, the width-three evaluator that the oracle module's `label` calls at 12 variables or fewer (model count, exact backbone, checked events). No definition case can occur: every clause has width three and `m >= 36`. The 72 cases at `n = 16, 20, 24` use the oracle under conflict limit `N = 100,000`, the only oracle limit. There are no composed calls; the call cap is zero and the answer file holds exactly one entry per oracle case, keyed by DIMACS digest, 72 entries expected. Sizing constraints: the per-entry proof cap is 2 MiB and the file cap 16 MiB. The largest committed LRAT proof in the repository is the fixture `tests/oracle/p60.lrat` for the 60-variable, 300-clause random formula `tests/oracle/r60.cnf`: 5,604 bytes, 89 lemmas, pinned by `tests/oracle.rs`; proofs at `n <= 24` are expected to be smaller, and even 72 entries of that size with their models are under 1 MB, far under the file cap, with no entry near the entry cap. The expectation matters: 72 entries near the entry cap would exceed the file cap, and regeneration would then refuse to write the file and the corpus would be reduced under a new protocol identity. The driver looks up a case's label only after all 28 arms have returned for it. A conflict-limit unknown is not expected at these sizes; if one occurs the case is kept, reported in every table, excluded from every comparison, and never re-drawn. No structured family with a theorem label is part of this corpus; if a later version adds one, it carries its label by construction with a cited proof and a structural check and is never sent to the oracle.

Acceptance per case: an arm outcome `solved` is verified by `check_model` on the checker meter outside all arms, with the charges `check_model_metered` in `src/oracle.rs` states (one formula check, one container write and `n` scalar writes for the assignment, one clause read per clause and one scalar read per literal examined); an unsatisfied clause, or a `solved` outcome on a case labelled `unsat`, invalidates the run. The truth-table and model checks charge on the checker meter as `implication::reference_wide` and `src/oracle.rs` implement them.

Per case the artifact records the object `{"id","seed","variables","density","clauses","input","initial_values","frozen_variables","label_bits","label_mod3","aggregation","initially_satisfied","setup_work","reference","solved_arms","check_model_work","trajectory_sha256","arms"}`: `id` is `agential-n<n>-d<d>-s<seed>` (for example `agential-n24-d426-s5003`), `input` the clauses in generation order as signed 1-based literals (the raw-clause list the ledger validator regenerates DIMACS from), `initial_values` the `n` values as 0/1 integers in variable order, `frozen_variables` the three drawn variables as 1-based indices in draw order, `label_bits` and `label_mod3` the `n` draws reduced to `r_i AND 1` and `r_i mod 3`, `aggregation` the two-way and three-way `A` as two `RatioPair`s over `n - 1`, `setup_work` the setup `Work` once per case, `reference` in the shape written by `src/oracle.rs` and described in `docs/oh.md`, `solved_arms` the number of `solved` arms, `check_model_work` the checker `Work` of their `check_model` calls summed, `trajectory_sha256` one SHA-256 over the concatenation of the 28 arms' serialized trajectories in arm order, and `arms` the 28 arm records in arm order, each: outcome, `rounds`, flips, flips per algotype, draws, total events, `D`, `X`, `N_dips`, and trajectory length.

## Part B: metrics

- **Success.** Per arm and per `(n, d)` cell, the number of `solved`, `stalled`, and `budget` outcomes over the cases labelled `sat`; cases labelled `unsat` are listed with their arm outcomes (`stalled` or `budget`, never `solved`) and their own stall and budget counts, and unknown-labelled cases with theirs, excluded from comparisons. `S(config, k)` is the pooled solved count over all `sat`-labelled cases in the corpus, with `stalled` and `budget` both counted as not solved; its denominator is the same for every arm, the number of `sat`-labelled cases, and only oracle-`unknown` cases are excluded. The budget count of every arm is reported beside every `S`.
- **Events per solved case.** Sum of charged events (setup plus search) over solved cases divided by the solved count, as a `RatioPair`, per arm and per `(n, d)`; the lower median as an integer. By category, the sum over solved cases and the sum over all cases are reported per arm pooled over the corpus as `Work` objects. Per pure configuration at `k = 0` and per `n`, the pooled median is the lower median of events over the solved `sat` cases pooled across the three densities at that `n`, `null` when there are none; the successive ratios of these medians across `n = 12 -> 16 -> 20 -> 24` are reported as `RatioPair`s, descriptive, finite range, no growth-class label.
- **Delayed gratification in `U`.** The trajectory `c_0 = m - U_0` before any flip, `c_j = m - U_j` after the `j`-th flip, serialized as decimal integers each followed by one `\n` including the last; the artifact stores its length per arm run and one digest per case over the 28 concatenated serializations, and a replay verifies every trajectory. Dips, `D`, `X`, `N_dips`, `R` exactly as in Part A, `R` recovered in the tables as `ratio_pair(X, D)`. Descriptive for every arm containing walk cells.
- **Aggregation over the index line.** `A = ` the number of `i` in `1..n-1` with equal labels of `i` and `i + 1`, over `n - 1`. Variables have no position that changes, so `A` is constant through a run and is reported once per case with its null expectation (`1/2` two-way; `1/3` three-way up to a bias of at most one part in 2^64 per draw, since `2^64 = 1 mod 3`). It is meaningless as an emergent statistic here: aggregation in Part A measures rearrangement of cells along the array, and the variable index is an arbitrary naming with no dynamics; the number is kept only so the port is complete. The descriptive quantity that does vary is the **flip share**: per mixed arm, each algotype's flips over all flips, beside its population share, both `RatioPair`s, pooled over the corpus.

## Part B: registered claims

All counts are over cases labelled `sat`; the only exclusion is an oracle-`unknown` label, reported with the number of cases excluded. A `budget` outcome is a completed run that did not solve the case inside the fixed budget and counts as not solved in every `S`; it never removes a case from a comparison. Every arm on every case completes with `solved`, `stalled`, or `budget`, so every comparison below is over the same set of cases for every arm compared. Claims are on the pooled corpus unless stated.

**B1, a chimeric mix lies between its components.** For each two-way mix `XY` at `k = 0`: `min(S(X), S(Y)) <= S(XY) <= max(S(X), S(Y))`, and for the three-way mix the same with the minimum and maximum over `G, W, C`; falsified by any violation of one case or more, reported with its margin. The same bracket on events per solved case is descriptive only, because the solved sets differ.

**B2, frozen variables reduce success monotonically.** For each of the seven configurations, `S(config, 0) >= S(config, 1) >= S(config, 2) >= S(config, 3)`, falsified by any increase; and for each pure configuration `S(config, 3) < S(config, 0)`, falsified by equality; equality at zero, when a configuration solves nothing at `k = 0`, falsifies this clause as a finding about the base rate rather than about damage and is reported as such. Frozen sets are nested in `k` by construction, so a single case can only lose solvability as `k` grows, but the deterministic dynamics can differ, which is what the claim tests.

**B3, ordering of the pure algotypes.** At `k = 0`: `S(W) >= S(G) >= S(C)`, falsified otherwise; expected from the nesting of flip conditions and walk's ability to leave a fixed point, and testable because every non-solved outcome, `stalled` or `budget`, counts against it.

**B4, density.** For `W` and `G` at `k = 0`, the solved fraction over `sat` cases at each density, pooled over the four values of `n` (numerator the solved `sat` cases at that density, denominator the `sat`-labelled cases at that density, at most 32), is nonincreasing across densities `300, 400, 426`, compared by cross-multiplication of the `RatioPair`s; falsified by any increase; a `null` fraction leaves the comparison at that density undecided, which cannot occur on this corpus unless all 32 cases at a density are unsat or unknown. The per-`(n, d)` fractions, whose cells hold eight cases, are descriptive.

Descriptive with no null: the per-`(n, d)` tables, the stall and budget counts, the `D` and `R` functionals of walk arms, the flip shares, and the successive ratios.

## Artifact layout and size

The ledger commits each observation part as its own operation (`scripts/oh-experiment-records.mjs`, `commitInParts`: `observations` is split into parts of at most 786,432 canonical bytes, each committed before the edition), so no future operation exceeds one part and a report under the 4,194,304-byte `MAX_REPORT_BYTES` pages part by part; the non-observation remainder of a report must stay under 786,432 canonical bytes because it goes whole into the edition record. This protocol nevertheless bounds the compact artifact at 1,800,000 bytes, the size of the largest edition the ledger has ingested (`ledger/pages/0003.json`, operation 15: the extraction-cost edition and its records, 1,801,809 canonical bytes in a 7,030,203-byte page), as the shared bound of this protocol set, and the layout below is sized to that bound by arithmetic at the widest decimal widths the rules allow, so the bound holds whatever the corpus does. The artifact's top-level `observations` array holds, in order, 93 Part A group rows (one per `(family, value family, algorithm or mix or pairing, damage configuration)` group: an identity object naming those four fields, then its 24 run records in seed order) followed by the 96 Part B case rows (each a case header with its 28 arm records); the remainder (header, interpretation statements, claim tables, per-arm and per-cell tables) is under 786,432 canonical bytes, an implementation check. The natural layout (per-run identity strings, ten-category `Work` objects per run, running-maximum profiles for every run, per-arm trajectory digests and category counters in Part B, 64-bit label draws) was sized at design time at 2.3 to 2.6 MB at those widths and is not used.

**Part A records.** Runs are stored in the 93 group rows of `observations` (84 `pure` groups: two value families, six algorithms, seven damage configurations; 6 `chimera` groups: two value families, three mixes; 3 `cross` groups), each `{"family","values","algorithm","damage","runs":[24 runs in seed order]}` with `"mix"` or `"pairing"` in place of `"algorithm"` and `"damage"` where the family has none, so no run carries an identity string; `"values"` is `unique` or `duplicate`, `"algorithm"`, `"mix"`, and `"pairing"` are the strings of the corpus section (`cv-bubble`, `bubble+insertion`, `bubble(-1)+selection(+1)`, and so on), and `"damage"` is the string `k0`, or `<kind><k>` (`movable1` to `immovable3`); the 93 identity objects are at most 92 bytes each without their `"runs"` key, 8,556 bytes. A run record is `{"outcome","rounds","swaps","denied","events":[total, scalar reads, scalar writes, pair checks, rule attempts],"final_error","dips":[D, X, N_dips],"aggregation":[A_peak, index, A_final],"trajectory":[length, sha256]}`; the five categories Part A never charges (formula checks, container reads and writes, assignments, search nodes) are zero in every run by construction (an implementation check) and are not stored. At the rule-bounded widths (rounds at most 5,000; swaps at most 4,950 in `pure` and `chimera`, at most 100,000 in `cross`; denied attempts at most two per activation; events at most 40,000,000; `D` and `X` at most four times the swaps because one swap changes `E` by at most four; `E` at most 99; trajectory length one more than the swaps; a 64-hex digest) a record is at most 280 bytes in `pure` cell-view and `chimera` runs, 289 in `cross`, and 260 in traditional runs: `1,152 x 280 + 72 x 289 + 1,008 x 260 = 605,448` bytes for 2,232 runs. The running-maximum profile `"profile":[[j, A_j], ...]` is stored only for the 216 runs that A3, its length-matched companion, and the cross family's descriptive reporting use: the 72 `unique` cell-view pure runs at `k = 0`, the 72 `unique` chimera runs, and the 72 `cross` runs. A profile has at most 100 entries; an entry is at most 10 bytes in `pure` and `chimera` (four-digit index) and 12 in `cross`, so the profiles are at most `144 x 1,011 + 72 x 1,211 = 232,776` bytes. Part A is at most 838,224 + 8,556 = 846,780 bytes. Recovery of dropped fields: a run's identity is its position in the tables; `S` is `ratio_pair(L - E, L)`; `R` is `ratio_pair(X, D)`; a profile absent from a run is recomputed by replay, and no claim uses it.

**Part B records.** A case header carries the fields listed in the corpus section; at `n = 24, m = 102` with an `unsat` oracle reference (the largest reference shape, with its proof-check `Work`) it is at most 3,183 bytes, with a `sat` reference 3,119, and at `n = 12` with a truth-table reference 2,818; bounding every oracle case by the larger shape, the 96 headers are at most `72 x 3,183 + 24 x 2,818 = 296,808` bytes. The 24 label draws are stored reduced (`label_bits`, `label_mod3`; 48 small integers rather than 24 twenty-digit integers); the draws themselves are regenerated from the seed, and the generator test pins their order. An arm record is `{"outcome","rounds","flips","draws","events","flips_by":[G, W, C],"dips":[D, X, N_dips],"trajectory_length"}`, at most 176 bytes at the rule-bounded widths (every count is under 200,000, the budget, including `D` and `X`, because a flip's drop is at most its occurrence count and each occurrence is a charged event; the figure was measured with `D` and `X` allowed seven digits, so it is not tight), so the `96 x 28 = 2,688` arm records are at most 473,088 bytes. Per-arm category counters are not stored per arm run; each arm's `Work` sums over solved `sat` cases and over all cases, pooled over the corpus (28 rows of at most 913 bytes, including the flip shares and the dip sums), and the per-`(arm, cell)` rows (336 rows of at most 191 bytes: the three outcome counts, the initially-satisfied count, the event sum over solved cases, the lower median, the events-per-solved `RatioPair`) are the tables, at most 106,540 bytes with an allowance for the pooled claim tables. Per-arm trajectory digests are replaced by one digest per case. Part B is at most 876,436 bytes. Recovery of dropped fields: per-arm category counters and per-arm trajectory digests are recomputed by replay, which verifies every trajectory against the per-case digest; the setup `Work` of every arm is the case's stored setup `Work`.

**Bound.** The report header, the interpretation statements, the Part A claim tables (A1 per algorithm and per `k`, A2's `D_k`, A3's and A5's 24 differences per mix with their sign counts and exact tails, A4's counts and brackets), and the Part B pooled claim tables are allotted 65,536 bytes, an implementation check. The total is at most `846,780 + 876,436 + 65,536 + 6,620 = 1,795,372` bytes, the last term the JSON separators the record widths exclude (`"runs":[]` and `"arms":[]` keys and the commas between records and rows), under the 1,800,000-byte bound by 4,628 bytes at the widest widths; the same layout at typical widths (four- to six-digit counts, profiles of about 20 entries) is about 1.3 MB. The artifact also stays under the ledger's 4,194,304-byte `MAX_REPORT_BYTES`. If the implemented artifact exceeds 1,800,000 bytes, the run is not published and the corpus is reduced under a new protocol identity; nothing is trimmed silently.

## Budgets, replay, and accounting

Every charged event is counted with checked arithmetic; every budget is a bound on charged events, never on time. Two figures are stated per part: the hard bound, which is the per-run budget times the run count, and the expected figure, which is derived from the design-time runs.

Part A: the hard bound is 2,232 runs times 40,000,000 events, 8.9 x 10^10, and is not the sizing basis; the per-run bounds derived from the rules and the design-time measured costs are.

Per-run bounds from the rules. A visit charges 1 event (stamp read) and an activation 3 more before its rule (stamp write, frozen read, rule attempt), 4 in all. A bubble rule then charges at most 8 (left read and check, a denied left attempt of one read, right read and check, a performed right attempt of one read and two writes), 12 in all at an interior position and 9 at `p = 0`; a selection rule at most 6 (target read, value read, check, performed attempt), 10 in all; an insertion rule at position `p >= 1` at most `2p + 3` (one read, `p - 1` scan steps of one read and one check each, one check, one performed attempt), `2p + 7` in all, and at `p = 0` nothing after the rule attempt, 4 in all. A selection cell at `p = 0` reads `t >= p`, so it clamps or ends and charges at most 6 in all; the per-position maximum over the three algotypes is therefore 9, 12, 12 at `p = 0, 1, 2` and `2p + 7` from `p = 3`, so a round charges at most `9 + 12 + 12 + sum_{p=3}^{99} (2p + 7) = 33 + 9,894 + 679 = 10,606` events on any array, at most `4 + sum_{p=1}^{99} (2p + 7) = 4 + 9,900 + 693 = 10,597` on an all-insertion array (with equality requiring every cell to be activated with a sorted prefix and a performed swap in the same round), and at most 1,200 when no cell is an insertion cell. A traditional run charges at most 35,244 events (bubble: at most 4,950 attempts of 7 events, two reads, one check, and a performed swap of two reads and two writes, plus 99 rule attempts, 34,749; insertion: the same 4,950 swap attempts of 7 plus, per outer iteration, one terminating step that is not a performed swap, a denied attempt of 3 + 2 events, and the rule attempt, 4,950 x 7 + 99 x 5 + 99 = 35,244; selection: 4,950 attempts of 4 plus 99 outer iterations of 5, 20,295). Consequently a `budget` outcome is impossible in every traditional run, in every cell-view run without insertion cells (at most 5,000 x 1,200 = 6,000,000 events), and in every `cross` run (at most 1,000 x 10,606 = 10,606,000 events); it is possible only in insertion-containing runs of the `pure` and `chimera` families, where 5,000 x 10,606 = 53,030,000 exceeds the budget. For pure `cv-insertion` and for `bubble+insertion`, every performed swap exchanges a strictly inverted adjacent pair and lowers the inversion count by exactly one, and every round but the last performs a swap (neither algotype writes a target), so the rounds are at most `I + 1` with `I` the initial inversion count, at most 4,950 (`unique`, all pairs of 100 positions) or 4,500 (`duplicate`, 4,950 less the 450 equal-value pairs); the per-run cost of pure `cv-insertion` is then at most 4,951 x 10,597 = 52,465,747 events, still above the budget, so the budget is the binding per-run bound for the 48 `cv-insertion` runs at `k = 0`, and the cell costs at most 48 x 40,000,000 = 1.92 x 10^9 events. No tighter bound for `cv-insertion` follows from the rules; the figures below are design-time measurements on the seeds named, not bounds.

Design-time measured costs (pre-freeze, corpus seeds). On corpus seed 7001, `cv-insertion` at `k = 0` cost 9,698,264 events on `unique` in 2,366 rounds (its largest round cost 10,306 against the all-insertion round bound of 10,597; `I = 2,457`), because each round's prefix scans cross the sorted stretch displaced by the one inserting cell, and 9,806,683 on `duplicate` in 2,347 rounds (`I = 2,432`); on seeds 7401 and 7801 the same runs cost 8,258,194 and 8,318,675, and 9,276,217 and 8,804,546, so the mean over the six design-time runs is 9,027,097 and the cell's expected total is 48 times that, 4.3 x 10^8. The six `cv-insertion` runs at `k >= 1` of one `(s, f)` cost together 41,246 (7001 `unique`, where every damaged run quiesced in 8 rounds under 7,000 events), 15,856,824 (7001 `duplicate`), 2,557,902 and 2,428,544 (7401 `unique` and `duplicate`), and 7,574,616 and 5,779,446 (7801): mean 5,706,430, expected total 48 times that, 2.7 x 10^8; early quiescence on one seed is not the rule. On seed 7001 the three shared-goal chimeras cost together 6,068,901 on `unique` (`bubble+insertion` 3,618,286 in 1,300 rounds, `insertion+selection` 2,369,836 in 1,195 rounds, `bubble+selection` 80,779 in 111 rounds) and 5,681,508 on `duplicate` (962, 1,311, and 105 rounds), expected total 24 times their sum, 2.8 x 10^8; the fourteen `cv-bubble` and `cv-selection` runs cost together 1,180,989 on `unique` and 803,115 on `duplicate`, every one under 110,000, with `cv-selection` at `k = 0` quiescent in 175 and 106 rounds, expected 24 times their sum, 4.8 x 10^7; the three `cross` runs on `unique` cost 650,812 (`bubble(-1)+selection(+1)`, capped at 1,000 rounds), 34,596, and 84,270, expected 24 times their sum, 1.8 x 10^7, against the family's rule bound of 24 x 1,200,000 + 48 x 10,606,000 = 5.4 x 10^8; the 1,008 traditional runs cost at most 1,008 x 35,244 = 3.6 x 10^7. Trajectory lengths on seed 7001 `unique`, quoted for A3's limitation: `cv-bubble` 2,458 entries, `bubble+selection` 1,902, `insertion+selection` 1,652, `cv-selection` 1,380. The expected Part A total is the sum of these six expectations, about 1.1 x 10^9 events, 36 s at 3 x 10^7 charged events per second in release; the cost of a run is a deterministic function of its seed and the rules, not of the machine.

Part B: the hard bound is 96 cases times 28 arms times 200,000 events, 5.4 x 10^8 (about 18 s), of which the 16 walk-containing arms account for 3.1 x 10^8 and the twelve others at most 96 x 12 x 113,462 = 1.3 x 10^8 by the rules; it is reached only if every walk-containing arm on every case exhausts its budget, and it is a bound, not an expectation. Sizing of the budget: over the design-time runs on the non-corpus seeds 911 to 914 and the corpus seeds 5003 and 10007, every solved walk-containing run used under 12,000 events and every stalled run under 5,000, so the budget is over sixteen times the largest solved cost seen. On corpus seeds 5003 and 10007 across the cells `(24, 426)`, `(24, 300)`, and `(16, 426)`, `W` exhausted its budget in 3 of 6 runs (`n = 24, d = 426` on both seeds and `n = 16, d = 426` on seed 10007), `GWC` in 2 of 6 (`n = 24, d = 300` on seed 10007, which cycled for 1,752 flips with `U` never rising, and `n = 16, d = 426` on seed 10007), and `G` and `C` never, every one of their runs ending under 5,000 events. At a budget-hit rate of one half on the walk-containing arms, the 768 budget runs cost 1.5 x 10^8 events and the 1,920 non-budget runs under 3 x 10^7 in total (1,920 x 12,000 = 2.3 x 10^7), so the expected Part B total is about 1.8 x 10^8 events (6 s).

Together, the expected replay is about 42 s (Part A 36 s, Part B 6 s) and the bound is 120 s on a laptop. The replay is designed to a ceiling of 3.6 x 10^9 charged events, which is 120 s at the assumed release rate, stated in events so that it is machine independent while the gate measures seconds: Part B's hard bound of 5.4 x 10^8 is reserved in full and Part A is allotted 3.06 x 10^9 against its expected 1.1 x 10^9. The rate of 3 x 10^7 events per second is a deliberately conservative assumption: the extraction-cost replay on one laptop charged 24.9 million events in 0.06 s in release, about 4 x 10^8 events per second, a design-time measurement committed nowhere. The ceiling does not rest on the dominant cell's design-time figures: with every `cv-insertion` run at `k = 0` at its 40,000,000-event budget (1.92 x 10^9), the rest of Part A at expectation (6.6 x 10^8), and Part B at its hard bound, the total is 3.1 x 10^9 events, 104 s. It does rest on the other insertion-containing cells of `pure` and `chimera` staying near their expectation, which three design-time seeds support and the rules bound only by the budget. Reference work is small: 24 truth tables of 4,096 rows, 72 model or LRAT checks over proofs expected under 10 KB each. If the implemented replay's Part A event total exceeds 3.06 x 10^9, or the measured replay exceeds 120 s, the corpus is reduced under a new protocol identity; nothing is trimmed silently.

The artifact carries no wall-clock seconds and no solver conflict counts; timing goes to the uncommitted sidecar `artifacts/agential-sorting-timing.json`. The report header declares `protocol_sha256` for this file, the reference model string, `"oracle_limits":{"conflicts":100000}`, `"proof_text_cap_bytes":2097152`, and `"oracle_answers":{"path":"artifacts/agential-sorting-oracle.json","sha256":"<file bytes>"}`, and the summary carries the oracle case, call, and label counts of `oracle::Summary` in `src/oracle.rs` (`truth_table_cases` 24, `definition_cases` 0, `oracle_cases` 72, `oracle_calls` 72, `oracle_sat`, `oracle_unsat`, `oracle_unknown`, `model_check_work_units`, `proof_check_work_units`, `proof_lemmas_total`, and, over the 24 truth-table cases, `reference_work_units` (the sum of the truth-table references' enumeration work) and `enumeration_ratio`, the `RatioPair` of that sum over the sum of the 28 arms' event totals on those same cases, descriptive). The artifact stays under the ledger's 4 MiB report bound and under this protocol's 1,800,000-byte compact bound by the layout section's arithmetic.

## Acceptance and reporting

A `solved` outcome whose model fails `check_model`, a `solved` outcome on an `unsat` label, a violated implementation check below, or a mismatch between a stored trajectory digest and its replay invalidates the calibration; repair implementation bugs transparently before publication, preserving the frozen design. Algorithm, corpus, budget, or criterion changes prompted by observed results require a new protocol identity. Keep every run's outcome, counters, functionals, and digests, every case's label with its certificate check, and every excluded case with the reason. Report Part A families in the order `pure`, `chimera`, `cross`, each with per-parameter subtotals, before Part B, and Part B per `(n, d)` cell before any pooled table. Negative results are reported as completely as positive ones; a falsified claim is reported with the same detail as a confirmed one. No required result is a positive delta.

**Implementation checks.** Each of the following follows from the rules and the fixed corpus; a violation is an implementation defect, never a result, and is resolved before publication with the frozen design preserved: every quiescent `bubble+insertion` run has `E = 0` (at the leftmost violation `i` the cell at `i` is either a bubble cell that would move left or an insertion cell whose prefix `0..i-1` is sorted and whose value is before its left neighbor, so it would move; and every swap of either algotype exchanges a strictly inverted adjacent pair, which reduces the inversion count, so the run terminates); no pure `cv-bubble`, pure `cv-insertion`, or `bubble+insertion` run is `capped`, and no `cv-bubble` run is `budget` (rounds at most 4,951, events at most 6,000,000); no `budget` outcome occurs in a traditional run, in a cell-view run without insertion cells, or in a `cross` run (their rule bounds lie below the budget); every round of every cell-view run charges at most 10,606 events, and at most 10,597 on an all-insertion array; a frozen cell or variable never moves or flips; every final array is a permutation of its input; a `budget` run's recorded event total equals the budget and its categories sum to it; the five categories Part A never charges are zero in every Part A run; `target <= position` holds throughout every pure `cv-selection` run; every Part B arm's setup total equals `15m + 3n + 2` and equals every other arm's on the same case; `N_dips = 0` in every `G`, `C`, and `GC` run; no `budget` outcome occurs in a `G`, `C`, or `GC` arm (each flip lowers `U` by at least one, so at most `m` flips and `m + 1` rounds of at most `7n + 9m` events each, at most 113,462 events with setup at `n = 24, m = 102`); an initially satisfied case is `solved` by all 28 arms with zero flips; the summary and claim tables occupy at most 65,536 bytes and the compact artifact at most 1,800,000 bytes; and no `solved` outcome occurs on a case whose initial assignment satisfies the formula except by the initially-satisfied rule.

Focused correctness tests include: each cell-view algotype alone on all 120 permutations of `1..5` and all 27 arrays of length 3 over `1..3` ends quiescent and nondecreasing (the theorem checks for pure runs); `bubble+insertion` on all permutations of `1..5` under all 32 label patterns ends sorted; the selection target invariant `target <= position` on pure runs; every frozen-kind combination on a two-cell inverted array under all six algorithms with exact charges, including the one-or-two-read cost of a denied traditional swap; a visited cell whose stamp equals the round charges one read and is not activated; a swap partner landing on the current position is not activated in that visit; the paper's Figure 6B example (legend pp. 29 to 30 of arXiv:2401.05375v1: in a short array the cell with value 3 cannot take the third position and swaps into the fourth), a cell blocked by an immovable neighbor, on a hand-built six-cell array of that shape; an insertion cell at position 99 with a sorted prefix charging exactly 2 x 99 + 7 = 205 events in one visit and activation; hand-built arrays of at least four cells on which the per-position charges 9, 12, 12 at `p = 0, 1, 2` of the round bound are each attained by one activation (a bubble cell at `p = 0` whose right swap is performed; a bubble cell at `p = 1` or `p = 2` whose left swap is denied by an immovable neighbor and whose right swap is performed), and a selection cell at `p = 0` charging at most 6; `E`, `S`, and `A` on hand arrays including the all-equal duplicate array; the running-maximum profile and `A_peak(l)` on a hand trajectory; dip extraction on `5,4,4,6,7,3,8` (two dips, drops 1 and 4, rises 3 and 5) and on a trajectory ending in a drop; the exact binomial tail for `N = 24` at `P = 24`, `P = 12`, and `P = 0`; Part B `makes` and `breaks` on hand clauses with duplicate clauses; the setup total `15m + 3n + 2` on a hand case; a hand case whose initial assignment satisfies the formula ending `solved` before round 1 with `rounds = 0`, zero flips, trajectory length 1, and its setup total as its events; a frozen variable never flipping; the `stalled` rule distinguishing a draw-free round from a round with draws; budget refusal inside a swap and inside a flip leaving the array a permutation, the assignment unchanged, the event total equal to the budget, and the trajectory digest consistent; the per-case Part B digest over a hand case's 28 concatenated trajectories; the compact record serialization of one Part A run and one Part B arm at the widest widths measuring at most 289 and 176 bytes; the demo replay fixture (`oracle::demo_corpus` with `tests/oracle/demo-oracle.json`) with one case of each label; and the generator's clause, value, frozen, and label draw order pinned by a stored digest of the first case's state.
